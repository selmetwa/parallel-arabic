## Commitment

*Let users declare their intent, then design around it.*

**What it is:** A micro-pledge from the user. A goal, schedule, or selection. You're inspiring behaviour by letting users make a commitment they will feel obliged to complete.

**Why it works:** Once we state our intentions, we feel the need to follow through. Even a small commitment public or private nudges us to act. Declarations create accountability which trigger consistency that leads to retention.

**When to use it:**
- When the product requires users to frequently check into the app
- To inspire activation in a demanding flow (e.g. learning, saving, tracking)

**Do:**
- Trigger follow-ups that tie back to what user has committed to
- Let users choose their own goal, frequency, or commitment
- Tie the commitment to future nudges, reminders, or feedback

**Don't:**
- Force arbitrary goals onto users
- Make the commitment feel like a irreversible trap
- Punish users for breaking commitments without flexibility

**Founder tip:** When users say what they want, help them become it. Apps that remember and reflect user intent build deeper loyalty and stickiness. Give users a goal and they'll have a purpose to retain.

**Pair with:** Personalisation · Gamified Progress · Time to Value · Deep-link · System Widget · Intent Mirroring

### Make It Yours

When users declare their intent, help them live up to it. These prompts help you turn commitment into structure, not pressure.

1. **To design the right kind of commitment**
   - What habit, outcome, or frequency matters most in your product?
   - How might a user pledge feel like progress, not pressure?
   - Could a lightweight goal or schedule boost activation or retention?
2. **To create space for intent**
   - Where in your flow could you ask, "What do you want out of this?"
   - Could users pick their own pace, metric, or end goal?
   - How might this commitment feel personal and empowering?
3. **To build follow-through loops**
   - Are we tying nudges, reminders, or tips to what the user said they wanted?
   - What check-ins could help users feel momentum not guilt?
   - How might we celebrate progress, even if they slip?
4. **To avoid coercion or shame**
   - Are we offering the choice to commit or forcing it?
   - Is there a clear way to update, edit, or back out of a goal?
   - Do users feel safe making imperfect progress?
5. **To test and evolve**
   - Are committed users more consistent or loyal?
   - Where do drop-offs happen after a pledge and why?
   - What signals suggest a user is ready to commit?

## Contact Bridge

*Connect with users' existing networks to enhance engagement and growth.*

**What it is:** Allow users to sync their phone contacts to instantly find friends or invite people they know. This lowers the barrier to early engagement and builds a sense of connection from day one.

**Why it works:** Syncing contacts at the right time unlocks instant social proof, lowers the barrier to connection, and leverages existing relationships to boost adoption.

**When to use it:**
- During onboarding to encourage immediate network building
- As part of referral campaigns to streamline invitations
- In social, productivity, or community-based apps where connections are core

**Do:**
- Show immediate value (e.g. "These 4 friends are already here")
- Highlight active friend counts to motivate connections
- Preload recognisable names or profile suggestions

**Don't:**
- Ask for permissions too early, before you've built trust
- Hide the purpose behind vague language ("Improve your experience...")

**Founder tip:** Use contact syncing as a gentle nudge to bridge offline relationships into the app. Highlight mutual connections to spark interest.

**Pair with:** Permission Serve · Success Moments · Time to Value · Deep-link · Discovery

### Make It Yours

We love to join where other people are. Use these prompts to lower the barrier to connection and boost engagement from day one.

1. **To time the ask**
   - When in your flow does a user most need encouragement to keep going?
   - Have we earned enough trust yet to ask for contact syncing?
   - What early win could we show immediately after they grant access?
2. **To frame the purpose**
   - Are we being direct and honest about why we want access?
   - Could we say, "See who's already here" instead of "Improve your experience"?
   - What specific benefit does contact syncing unlock for the user right now?
3. **To surface meaningful connections**
   - Can we preload recognisable names or avatars?
   - Could we show live activity ("X just joined" or "Y is active")?
   - How might we use social proof to encourage interaction not just invite spamming?
4. **To reduce friction and discomfort**
   - Is it obvious what the user is agreeing to and what happens next?
   - Are we giving users full control (skip, edit, or limit syncing)?
   - Could we delay the ask until there's a clear reason to connect?
5. **To test and adapt**
   - What % of users who sync contacts take a next step?
   - Are we seeing higher retention among connected users?
   - Where do users bounce at the permission request, or after?

## Deep-link

*Drop users into the exact moment, feature, or context that matters.*

**What it is:** The ability to direct users attention to a specific screen, state, or flow inside your product, bypassing layers of navigation.

**Why it works:** It respects user intent by reducing effort and confusion. It points users directly to the tasks maintaining focus on the task.

**When to use it:**
- Returning user picking up where they left off
- To inspire activation in a demanding flow (e.g. learning, saving, tracking)
- In user-to-user sharing or referrals

**Do:**
- Route users to the exact context relevant to the message or CTA
- If sending users out of the app, guide them back into where they left
- Use deep-links to bridge channels (email → app, web → app)

**Don't:**
- Dump users on a generic page after promising something specific
- Forget to test for logged-out users, edge cases, or navigational detours

**Founder tip:** Deep links are portals to value. Respect your user's time and attention by helping them find the exact flow instantly.

**Pair with:** Time to Value · Commitment · Gamified Progress · Contact Bridge · Discovery · Limited Offer · Referral

### Make It Yours

When users expect specificity, don't send them searching. Use these prompts to drop them exactly where value lives.

1. **To define where deep-links should lead**
   - What's the most valuable or actionable place to drop a returning user?
   - Are there moments in your app that are buried too deep behind menus?
   - What parts of your product are commonly referenced in emails, messages, or support?
2. **To ensure contextual continuity**
   - Could this link pick up where the user left off?
   - Does this entry point match the promise made in the message or CTA?
   - How might we maintain flow across devices or platforms?
3. **To optimise the experience**
   - What happens if a logged-out user clicks this deep-link?
   - Are we testing edge cases like expired sessions, deleted content, or outdated links?
   - Can we tailor the screen users land on depending on their intent or goal?
4. **To increase trust and activation**
   - Does this feel like a personal shortcut or a marketing trap?
   - Could we use deep-links to onboard a user into the right moment, not just the home screen?
   - Are we nudging users toward action, not just information?
5. **To evaluate effectiveness**
   - Where do users most often drop off after clicking in?
   - Are we measuring how many users actually complete the task from a deep-link?
   - What kinds of deep-links perform best, returning, shared, or cross-channel?

## Discovery

*Guide users to value before they know where to look.*

**What it is:** Surfacing helpful content, tools, or features before the user begins searching. It anticipates action and replaces decision paralysis with meaningful suggestions.

**Why it works:** By highlighting what's possible, discovery reduces cognitive load and replaces "blank canvas anxiety" with curiosity. When users see what's possible, they explore, stay longer, and reach value faster.

**When to use it:**
- During onboarding or in early sessions when users don't know what's possible
- In search UIs, dashboards, or category pages
- When introducing new tools or features
- Anywhere users benefit from being demonstrated possibilities

**Do:**
- Use dynamic suggestions powered by user behaviour, trends or context
- Seed exploration with "Most popular," "You might like," or "People also used.."
- Show relevant queries before users type in search

**Don't:**
- Overwhelm with too many suggestions it becomes irrelevant noise
- Neglect users without introducing them through guided discovery

**Founder tip:** Discovery is a trust-building loop. The more helpful your suggestions are, the more users believe your product "gets them" making them likely to stay and explore deeper.

**Pair with:** Empty States · Contact Bridge · Spark Curiosity · Time to Value · Referral · The Paywall

### Make It Yours

Don't wait for users to search. Use these prompts to help your product suggest before it's requested and turn curiosity into connection.

1. **To identify the moment of need**
   - Where are users most likely to feel unsure or stuck?
   - Are there moments in the flow that feel like a dead end?
   - When do users pause, hesitate, or bounce and why?
2. **To design helpful suggestions**
   - Could we suggest the next best action based on what they just did?
   - What's trending, popular, or proven that could seed exploration?
   - Are we showing "what's possible" or just "what's available"?
3. **To personalise discovery**
   - What signals (e.g. scrolls, clicks, skips) are we using to tailor suggestions?
   - Can we mirror behaviour (e.g. "Others who tried X also liked Y")?
   - Are we offering suggestions before users start typing?
4. **To reduce cognitive load**
   - Is our UI calm, scannable, and easy to act on?
   - Are we overwhelming users with too many options or too much noise?
   - Could we break suggestions into categories that reduce decision anxiety?
5. **To keep discovery alive**
   - Do suggestions evolve with user behaviour and trends?
   - Are we highlighting new or timely content that sparks curiosity?
   - Is discovery working like a loop or just a one-time nudge?

## Effort Moat

*Turn invested effort into loyalty by acknowledging it.*

**What it is:** Resurface the user's investment time, energy, or emotion to make their effort feel valuable and hard to abandon.

**Why it works:** People value what they build. When a user uploads, completes tasks, or configures their space, they create psychological "ownership" that increases attachment.

**When to use it:**
- After setup flows, saved preferences, or input-heavy tasks
- During inactivity, to reignite personal momentum
- After they've built a personalised experience (e.g. saved places, uploads, notes, history)

**Do:**
- Show cumulative stats and progress ("You've saved 27 favourites")
- Reflect input back visually. Dashboards, saved states, timelines
- Elevate earned status (e.g. streaks, social badges, ranks)

**Don't:**
- Let effort go unacknowledged, neglect leads to disconnection
- Hide user's effort once they have made even a small contribution

**Founder tip:** When users build personal curations e.g. saved spots in Google Maps they develop subconscious switching resistance. This becomes your moat. Take it further: if their contributions lead to social recognition or status, you've helped them build identity. That's loyalty at the highest tier.

**Pair with:** Success Moments · Gamified Progress · Value Replay · Investment · Time to Value

### Make It Yours

We get attached to what we've built. Use these prompts to reflect effort, reinforce identity, and build psychological switching costs.

1. **To spot invested effort**
   - Where in your product do users input, organise, or personalise content?
   - What actions require repeated effort whether cognitive, emotional, or time-based?
   - What setup steps or preferences could become visible proof of progress?
2. **To make investment visible**
   - Could we reflect their input back as stats, timelines, or saved states?
   - What streaks, badges, or social indicators could elevate their status?
   - Are we showing how far they've come in a way that feels earned?
3. **To create emotional attachment**
   - Where can we highlight user-created value (e.g. notes, uploads, collections)?
   - Could we let them name, tag, or personalise part of the product?
   - How might we make their setup feel like "their version" of the app?
4. **To avoid invisibility**
   - Are users completing tasks without seeing the impact?
   - Could we summarise their contributions in a digest, dashboard, or empty state?
   - Are we unintentionally hiding or deleting signs of investment?
5. **To validate and deepen the moat**
   - Which users have the highest effort footprint and do they churn less?
   - What's the average time or number of inputs before retention improves?
   - How can we give users a sense of progress even from passive use?

## Empty States

*Turning blank screens into activation opportunities.*

**What it is:** Empty states appear when there's no content to show yet. Instead of leaving users stranded, they're your chance to guide, inspire, and onboard.

**Why it works:** A blank screen is a dead end. But a clear, helpful empty state nudges action, sets expectations, and turns nothing into momentum. It's your first impression and a chance to make understanding the next steps easy for users.

**When to use it:**
- When a page would otherwise be blank: after signup, first use, or clearing content
- When filters or search open, also when they return no results
- During onboarding flows where input is expected but users lack context

**Do:**
- Add a short headline & helpful microcopy that explains what to do next
- Show what good looks like use illustrations, examples, or demos
- Align tone with your brand voice friendly, clear, never robotic

**Don't:**
- Leave the screen empty or cryptic ("No items found." is not enough)
- Assume users know what to do without instruction
- Make empty states static, they should be a prompt to inspire action

**Founder tip:** Every empty state is a chance to teach, inspire, and build trust. The first tap forward starts here. A great empty state can trigger activation.

**Pair with:** JTBD Copywriting · Setup Defaults · Time to Value · Progressive Disclosure · Discovery

### Make It Yours

A blank screen is either a dead end or an open door. Use these questions to make sure your empty states invite action not confusion.

1. **To recognise where empty states exist**
   - Which screens in your app show "nothing" when users first arrive?
   - What happens when filters or searches return no results?
   - Are there parts of the onboarding journey that assume user input before anything appears?
2. **To frame the opportunity**
   - What action do you want the user to take in this moment?
   - What fear, doubt, or question might they have while looking at this empty state?
   - Could this be the first time they're seeing your product, what impression are you leaving?
3. **To design with clarity and care**
   - What's the one message users need to hear right now to take action?
   - Can you add a visual cue or example of what "good" looks like?
   - How might you teach through the UI instead of relying on tooltips or docs?
4. **To align with brand and flow**
   - What tone matches your brand: instructional, playful, confident?
   - Could this screen reflect your product's core value in a small, immediate way?
   - How might the empty state feel like progress rather than a void?
5. **To evolve and test**
   - Are users bouncing off this screen or taking the intended next step?
   - What questions or support requests come from users stuck here?
   - Could a revised empty state increase conversion or onboarding success?

## Fail Safe

*Destructive actions are guarded with increased effort.*

**What it is:** Designing critical actions (like deletions, payments, submissions) with thoughtful friction or undo options so users can act confidently without the fear of irreversible mistakes.

**Why it works:** It reduces anxiety, especially for new users, and encourages exploration. People are more likely to try new features when they know they can't break anything.

**When to use it:**
- For irreversible or high-stakes actions (delete account, send payment, submit form)
- When designing "danger zones" or admin panels
- Use clear, emotionally honest language (e.g. "This can't be undone")

**Do:**
- Add intentional friction to risky actions (confirmation, double-tap, hold-to-confirm)
- Offer soft-deletes, undo snackbars, or grace windows
- Provide contextual language that reinforces the risk ("This action can't be undone")
- Use progress saving or auto-draft saving

**Don't:**
- Let destructive actions fire with a single careless tap
- Hide risk behind familiar patterns
- Assume users understand the stakes, always clarify impact

**Founder tip:** Fail-safes are invisible trust signals. When users know they're safe, they act with confidence. Reduce support tickets and churn caused by accidental actions.

**Pair with:** Micro Interactions · JTBD Copywriting · Intentional Friction

### Make It Yours

How are we protecting users from permanent regret? Use these prompts to design with care, not just caution.

1. **To identify risk points**
   - Which actions in our product are truly irreversible?
   - Are there any "danger zones" users might stumble into too easily?
   - Where are we seeing the most support tickets or accidental churn?
2. **To reinforce thoughtful control**
   - Do users understand why this action matters before committing?
   - Are we using plain, direct language at the moment of risk?
   - Could an "undo" action or soft delete be safer than a hard confirmation?
3. **To reduce hesitation (not flow)**
   - Are we slowing users just enough to create clarity, not frustration?
   - Can we add save states, drafts, or warnings to reduce decision pressure?
   - Do users feel like they can explore safely without breaking anything?
4. **To make confidence the default**
   - Is the system communicating that they're in control?
   - Are we protecting users silently (auto-saving, contextual warnings)?
   - Are destructive actions hidden behind misleading icons or gestures?

## Gamified Progress

*Game mechanics that trigger habit loops.*

**What it is:** Missions, milestones, or visual feedback which reward user actions and create a sense of forward motion. These structures mimic game loops for purposeful progress.

**Why it works:** Progress is addictive. Even small wins build a sense of achievement, purpose, and momentum. Game-like systems trigger reward circuits and reinforce behaviour. The right loops keep users engaged and keep them coming back.

**When to use it:**
- Multi-step processes, habit-building features, onboarding, daily tasks
- When you want to increase stickiness with repeat use
- To break long tasks into motivating, bite-sized steps
- When your product has daily/weekly engagement

**Do:**
- Make the first few steps fast and achievable momentum early on matters most
- Use missions or quests to frame progress with purpose
- Add micro-rewards (sounds, animations, feedback) that support and feel earned

**Don't:**
- Force gamification into flows that don't benefit from it
- Use meaningless badges or empty point systems. Reward must feel relevant

**Founder tip:** Don't treat gamification as an add on. it's a behavioural engine, Use it to guide behaviour and build rituals. When building it, consider the B=MAP framework.

**Pair with:** Success Moments · Micro Interactions · System Widget · Time to Value · Commitment · Shareability · Momentum Bias

### Make It Yours

Use these prompts to design habit loops, unlock progress, and drive return use.

1. **Where does progress matter most?**
   - Are users completing a multi-step flow or setup?
   - Are we asking them to build habits, track goals, or hit milestones?
   - Where do users typically drop off and could progress nudge them to continue?
2. **What does "winning" look like in your product?**
   - Can we define what success looks like for a session, day, or goal?
   - Is there a repeatable loop we want users to complete?
   - What are the early wins we can surface to build momentum?
3. **How do we make progress feel visible and satisfying?**
   - Are we showing users how far they've come and what's left?
   - Are our cues (bars, steps, streaks) designed with emotion and clarity?
   - Can we celebrate progress with micro-interactions, sounds, or animations?
4. **Is our progress loop designed for return behaviour?**
   - Will users come back to maintain a streak or check off a goal?
   - Could we surface daily challenges, quests, or evolving rewards?
   - Are we creating urgency or curiosity to re-engage?
5. **Are we reinforcing meaningful effort?**
   - Does progress align with outcomes the user cares about?
   - Are rewards earned or just decorative?
   - Are we building emotional investment with every checkmark?

## Intent Mirroring

*Reflect user behavior in real time to build trust and drive action.*

**What it is:** A responsive UX pattern that reacts to subtle user behaviours, like pauses, backtracking, or repeated filters by surfacing timely nudges, guidance, or content that aligns with their likely intent.

**Why it works:** We feel seen when an app responds to actions. Mirroring takes user behaviour and surfaces relevant actions based on intent. This builds trust and reduces effort. It makes the app feel helpful.

**When to use it:**
- When users are browsing, hesitating, or stuck in loops
- During onboarding, pricing exploration, or filter-heavy search
- In moments of hesitation (e.g. hovering, scroll-backs, or long pauses)

**Do:**
- Surface relevant help ("Still comparing plans?") when patterns are clear
- Use micro-signals to adapt UI, offer a shortcut, template, or filtered result
- Match the user's current mode: explore, decide, or compare

**Don't:**
- Surface suggestions too early, this can feel like noise if irrelevant to intent
- Offer generic nudges that break the illusion of personalisation

**Founder tip:** Intent mirroring is co-navigation. You're anticipating. When done right, it's invisible UX that feels magical. The user thinks, "That's exactly what I needed."

**Pair with:** Personalisation · Pattern Alignment · Progressive Disclosure · Time to Value

### Make It Yours

When users hesitate, your product should respond. Use these prompts to make your UX feel intelligent, empathetic, and aligned.

1. **To detect intent**
   - Where are users pausing, hovering, or scrolling back?
   - What filters, inputs, or screens are users repeating or spending time on?
   - Which behaviours signal interest but not action?
2. **To surface smart nudges**
   - Could we ask a question based on what they've been doing? ("Still comparing plans?")
   - What small hint, autofill, or suggestion might guide them forward?
   - Are we reinforcing their curiosity or pushing them?
3. **To adapt without intrusion**
   - How might we reflect behaviour without stealing attention?
   - Could this response feel like a natural continuation and not a pop-up?
   - Are we matching tone and timing to their apparent mindset?
4. **To avoid overreach**
   - Are we interrupting too soon or personalising too much, too fast?
   - Could this behaviour mean multiple things and are we assuming the wrong one?
   - Is our suggestion genuinely helpful or just trying to convert?
5. **To measure impact**
   - Does mirroring improve conversion or shorten time to value?
   - Where do users bounce after interacting with a mirrored suggestion?
   - Are users describing the product as helpful, relevant, or "it just got me"?

## Intentional Friction

*Introduction of purposeful slowdown to drive clarity, confidence, or commitment.*

**What it is:** Strategically placed friction in a flow not to block, but to pause and think. It's increased effort by design. The goal isn't to avoid moving faster, it's to move with purpose.

**Why it works:** It helps users make better decisions, prevents mistakes, and improves input quality.

**When to use it:**
- High-stakes actions like deleting data or making purchases
- Limiting features before converting users through a paywall
- Requiring user input to build a personalised experiences

**Do:**
- Time the friction by first providing context
- Reveal the optimised experience once user converts
- Create moments that educate, protect, or deepen commitment

**Don't:**
- Add unnecessary friction that doesn't help user progress
- Assume all friction is bad; context matters
- Add friction without purpose or feedback

**Founder tip:** Not all friction is bad. Incorporating friction isn't about making tasks harder it's about making experiences thoughtful and guided to reveal the payoff a user will value.

**Pair with:** Time to Value · Setup Defaults · Progressive Disclosure · Variable Reward · Fail Safe · Perceived Effort Delay

### Make It Yours

Friction isn't the enemy, confusion is. Use these prompts to slow the flow with intention, clarity, and care.

1. **To find the right moment for friction**
   - Where in your flow is speed causing mistakes or regret?
   - Are users skipping steps they should consider more carefully?
   - Which decision points could benefit from a pause or checkpoint?
2. **To frame friction as value**
   - What can we teach, clarify, or reassure the user of in this moment?
   - How might this friction build trust, not resistance?
   - Could we reveal a better experience after this step to reward the effort?
3. **To calibrate effort**
   - Does this step feel appropriately challenging or just annoying?
   - What would make this feel like a meaningful choice, not a roadblock?
   - Are we asking for effort at the right time in the journey?
4. **To avoid misuse**
   - Are we adding steps because we want control or because users need clarity?
   - Could we combine two moments of friction into one smoother step?
   - Is this friction backed by a clear purpose or outcome?
5. **To iterate and evolve**
   - Where are users dropping off, could friction or the lack off be to blame?
   - Could micro-feedback help users feel progress during the pause?
   - What does success look like after friction is introduced?

## Investment

*User input that deepens attachment and improves the product.*

**What it is:** A product pattern that asks users to input time, effort, or decisions like choosing preferences, saving data, or generating content. These acts of participation increase ownership. The more users invest, the more valuable the product feels.

**Why it works:** We value what we shape. Every small contribution becomes a sunk cost that deepens emotional and cognitive commitment. This creates product gravity. It also sharpens the product's relevance, building stronger feedback loops.

**When to use it:**
- During onboarding, when users are forming habits and mental models
- When storing user data improves future recommendations or value
- To build a sense of progress before surfacing a paywall or locked feature

**Do:**
- Give users a reason to come back by connecting input to future reward
- Use stored actions to make the next visit easier, faster, or more delightful
- Highlight growing investment ("You've logged 9 entries, " "Next unlock in 2 uploads")

**Don't:**
- Ask for input without context and benefits
- Let data disappear, visible investment creates attachment
- Interrupt the feedback loop by resetting progress or deleting effort

**Founder tip:** Users are loyal to what they've shaped. Every upload, setting, or saved item makes your product feel like their product. Leverage input not just for personalisation, but to power future triggers and stronger retention.

**Pair with:** Value Replay · The Paywall · Progressive Disclosure · Time to Value · Personalisation

### Make It Yours

People protect what they build. Use these prompts to design in-product actions that grow ownership, raise return potential, and deepen attachment.

1. **Spot investment triggers**
   - What actions require time, effort, input, or decisions from the user?
   - Are users asked to share preferences, create content, or upload data?
   - Where do users leave behind emotional, social, or identity-based signals?
2. **Turn effort into attachment**
   - What visible signs show the user what they've built so far?
   - Could we auto-save, personalise, or preview based on their input?
   - What would make this feel more like their space, not just the app?
3. **Use progress to motivate return**
   - Do we give early signs of impact or reward (e.g. saved streaks, previews)?
   - Is there a reason for users to return, to finish, improve, or protect progress?
   - Could this input trigger smarter defaults or reminders next session?
4. **Avoid invisible effort**
   - Are we asking users to contribute before they've seen value?
   - Could input get lost or reset unexpectedly?
   - Is there input friction with no emotional payoff?
5. **Connect input to insight**
   - What types of user input improve product recommendations?
   - How does stored effort (uploads, edits, favourites) sharpen UX or retention?
   - Are we feeding that back to the user with clarity?

## JTBD Copywriting

*Choose words that move users forward with clarity and purpose.*

**What it is:** Conversion-focused copy that frames actions through the user's desired outcome. It shifts from describing what a feature does to showing what the user can achieve with it.

**Why it works:** People don't want tools. They want results. When words reflect their intention it creates momentum. JTBD copy removes friction, builds confidence, and keeps users oriented around their goal.

**When to use it:**
- Everywhere, buttons, onboarding steps, tooltips, error states
- Anywhere you need to prompt action, calm hesitation, or frame next steps

**Do:**
- Frame tasks as outcome-driven actions
- Match voice to mindset: reassuring where there's hesitation, warm during effort
- Foreshadow what comes next give a sense of control and purpose

**Don't:**
- Use jargon or overly complicated phrases
- Describe the features of the app
- Use generic CTAs like "Submit" or "Continue" where context could work

**Founder tip:** Every word in your UI is a tiny nudge. Default to the user's why, not your what. A good test: read it aloud after "Now you can...", if it doesn't make sense, rewrite it.

**Pair with:** Micro Interactions · Empty States · Loading Feedback · Permission Serve · Fail Safe

### Make It Yours

Copy isn't just what you say, its how you and when you say it. Use these questions to write words that guide, empower, and convert.

1. **To understand the user's job**
   - What outcome is the user trying to achieve on this screen?
   - What emotion are they likely feeling right now uncertainty, ambition, relief?

   - Would a delayed reveal heighten anticipation?

## The Paywall

- To break monotony without changing structure (e.g. new visuals, tips, or bonuses)

- Make the randomness feel rewarding, not arbitrary
- Use in flows where outcomes aren't mission-critical
- Highlight differences visually (badges, colours, rare finds, rotating tips)

**Don't:**
- Add randomness where users expect control or reliability
- Hide important functionality behind randomness
- Create loops that feel addictive without purpose

**Founder tip:** A small variable twist can make users lean in. Keep them coming back and interact with the exciting content again.
