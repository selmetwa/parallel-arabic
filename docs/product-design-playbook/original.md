Introduction Built for founders & early- stage teams that want to leverage the best design practices to scale their app 

Users don’t just remember what your app does. They remember how it feels. This playbook gives you a founding-designer lens to map end- to-end flows that onboard, activate, retain, and monetise.    

Turn scattered features into one coherent experience and design moments that deliberately guide behaviour toward measurable outcomes.    

Whether you’re chasing PMF or fine-tuning an existing product, it’s your edge for making better decisions

1\. Activation Rate  

Formula: (Users who complete core action ÷ Total signups) × 100  What it measures: Percentage of new users who reach first value  Why it matters: High activation means your onboarding leads to meaningful engagement. Low activation signals friction, confusion, or a weak first impression.   

Time to Value (TTV)  Formula: Average time from signup to first core action  What it measures: How quickly users experience product value  Why it matters: The faster the TTV, the less drop-off. Speed is retention’s friend.   

Onboarding Completion Rate  Formula: (Users who finish all onboarding steps / Users who started) × 100  What it measures: How many users make it through the onboarding sequence.  Why it matters: Completion signals that the flow was clear, motivating, and relevant.   

Step-by-Step Drop-off Rate  Formula: (Users who dropped off at step N / Users who reached step N) × 100  What it measures: Where users abandon the onboarding flow  Why it matters: It reveals exactly the point of friction where users abandon the task.   

Day 1/7/30 Retention  Formula: (Users active on day X / Users who signed up X days ago) × 100  What it measures: Whether onboarding built enough momentum to bring users back.  Why it matters: Retention is the long tail of onboarding. If you get Day 1 wrong, Day 30 doesn’t matter.

1\. Free-to-Paid Conversion Rate  

Formula: (Paid subscribers / Total free users) × 100  What it measures: The percentage of free users who become paying customers.  Why it matters: It shows how well your product turns interest into income   

Paywall Conversion Rate  Formula: (Users who upgraded / Users who saw paywall) × 100  What it measures: Effectiveness of paywall presentation  Why it matters: It captures how persuasive your offer is at the decision moment   

Average Revenue Per User (ARPU)  Formula: Total revenue / Total active users (free \+ paid)  What it measures: Revenue generated per user over time period  Why it matters: Helps you assess the overall monetisation health across your base   

Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)  Formula: (ARPU × Gross margin %) / Churn rate  What it measures: Total revenue expected from a customer  Why it matters: Shows total revenue each customer brings in before they churn   

Trial-to-Paid Conversion Rate  Formula: (Trial users who converted / Total trial users) × 100  What it measures: How effectively trials drive subscriptions  It reflects the strength of your product experience   

Churn Rate  Formula: (Customers lost / Total customers at start of period) × 100  What it measures: Rate of subscription cancellations  Why it matters: Signals how quickly you're losing paying customers over time.

􀉟 Pla Subtle feedback loops that make digital products feel tactile and alive. 

What it is:  Small, functional animations triggered by user actions. Taps, swipes, hovers, holds, or transitions that reinforce the cause-and-effect loop of interaction.   

Why it works:  They confirm intent, reduce uncertainty, and inject life into the interface. When done right, they make your product feel responsive, tactile, and thoughtfully built.   

When to use it:  

On user actions: taps, clicks, drag & drops  During transitions between states or screens  On success, error, or confirmation moments  As ambient feedback during navigation or loading   

Do: Use micro-interactions to confirm the app is responding  

Add subtle animation to reinforce feedback loops and user flow  Use haptics sparingly to enhance key moments  Let users toggle haptics in settings for control and accessibility   

Don’t:  

Over animate basic interactions. Too much movement becomes noise  Use animations that delay the experience or feel gimmicky  Use complex sequences that slow down flow.   

Founder Tip:  Small polish often feels premium quality to users but only when used intentionally. If your product doesn’t feel alive, and responsive, it feels broken.

If your product doesn’t feel alive, it risks feeling broken. Use these prompts to fine-tune where, why, and how your product should respond to users. 

To identify high-impact touchpoints:  

Where do users need reassurance that their action was received?  What moments in your product feel static, cold, or uncertain?  Are there actions where users hesitate, wondering if the app is still working?   

2\. To choose the right interaction type:  

Would a subtle nudge (vibration, bounce, shimmer) clarify what just happened?  How might you signal state change (success, error, transition) with motion instead of words?  What kind of feedback would feel native to your product: visual, haptic, or both?   

3\. To avoid going overboard:  

Where does motion currently get in the way of speed or clarity?  Are there animations that feel playful... but slow users down?  How might we remove animation that adds noise, not value?   

4\. To align with brand and tone:  

What does motion look like for your brand? Is it snappy, smooth, playful, or minimal?  Could a signature micro-interaction become part of your product’s identity?  Where might delight be better expressed as subtle craft rather than big theatrics?
