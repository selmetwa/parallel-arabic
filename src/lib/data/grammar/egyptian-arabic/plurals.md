Arabic makes plurals in two ways: by adding an ending (a "sound" plural), or by changing the vowels inside the word (a "broken" plural).

## Sound plurals: add an ending

**-iin** is mostly for male people and occupations:

```ex
مدرس ← مدرسين | mudarris → mudarrisiin | teacher → teachers
مهندس ← مهندسين | muhandis → muhandisiin | engineer → engineers
```

**-aat** is for most feminine nouns (drop the **-a** first), and for many borrowed words:

```ex
عربية ← عربيات | 3arabiyya → 3arabiyyaat | car → cars
محطة ← محطات | ma7atta → ma7attaat | station → stations
تليفون ← تليفونات | tilifoon → tilifoonaat | phone → phones
```

## Broken plurals: the word changes shape

Most everyday nouns have a broken plural. The root letters stay, but the vowels between them change.

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كتاب kitaab | كتب kutub | book |
| بيت beet | بيوت buyuut | house |
| ولد walad | ولاد wilaad | boy |
| بنت bint | بنات banaat | girl |
| راجل raagil | رجالة rigaala | man |
| كلب kalb | كلاب kilaab | dog |
| شباك shibbaak | شبابيك shababiik | window |

There are patterns (CuCuC, CiCaaC, CuCuuC…), but no reliable way to predict which one a noun takes. Learn each plural together with its singular.

## Collective nouns: apples, trees, eggs

Many foods, plants and animals have a "collective" form that means the stuff in general. Add **-a** to get one item, and **-aat** to count several.

```ex
تفاح | tuffaa7 | apples (in general)
تفاحة | tuffaa7a | one apple
تلات تفاحات | talat tuffaa7aat | three apples
```

> Numbers change which form you use: 3 to 10 take the plural, but 11 and up go back to the singular. See [counting things](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/numbers-counting).
