An Egyptian present-tense verb has three parts: a **person prefix** that shows who is doing it, the **verb stem**, and usually a **b-** in front meaning "now" or "usually".

## The bare present

Each person has a prefix, and some also have an ending. Here is **katab** (to write), stem **-ktib**:

| Person | Bare form | With b- |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | aktib | baktib |
| inta (you m) | tiktib | bitiktib |
| inti (you f) | tiktibi | bitiktibi |
| huwwa (he) | yiktib | biyiktib |
| hiyya (she) | tiktib | bitiktib |
| i7na (we) | niktib | biniktib |
| intu (you pl) | tiktibu | bitiktibu |
| humma (they) | yiktibu | biyiktibu |

Notice that "you (m)" and "she" are the same form. Context, or the pronoun, tells them apart.

## When to use bi-

**bi-** is the normal present: things happening now, or habits.

```ex
بكتب جواب | baktib gawaab | I'm writing a letter.
بيشتغل في بنك | biyishtaghal fi bank | He works in a bank.
```

## When to drop bi-

Use the bare form after words like "want", "must", "can" and "let's". English uses "to" + verb here; Arabic uses the bare present with the right person.

```ex
عايز أكتب | 3aayiz aktib | I want to write.
لازم تيجي | laazim tiigi | You have to come.
ممكن نتكلم؟ | mumkin nitkallim? | Can we talk?
يلا نروح | yalla nruu7 | Let's go.
```

There is no separate infinitive ("to write") in Egyptian: the verb always carries a person.

## Movement verbs use a participle

For "going", "coming" and similar, Egyptians usually use an adjective-like participle instead of **bi-**:

```ex
أنا رايح الشغل | ana raayi7 ish-shughl | I'm going to work.
هي جاية دلوقتي | hiyya gaaya dilwa2ti | She's coming now.
```

> For "will", put **ha-** in front of the bare form. See [the future](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/future).
