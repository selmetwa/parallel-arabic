The past tense is the simplest Arabic tense: one stem, with an ending for each person.

## Endings

Here is **katab** (he wrote):

| Person | Ending | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | -t | katabt |
| inta (you m) | -t | katabt |
| inti (you f) | -ti | katabti |
| huwwa (he) | (none) | katab |
| hiyya (she) | -it | katabit |
| i7na (we) | -na | katabna |
| intu (you pl) | -tu | katabtu |
| humma (they) | -u | katabu |

"He" has no ending, so it's the form you'll find in dictionaries and in the verb tables.

```ex
كتبت الجواب امبارح | katabt ig-gawaab imbaari7 | I wrote the letter yesterday.
هي خرجت بدري | hiyya kharagit badri | She went out early.
شربنا شاي | sharabna shaay | We drank tea.
```

## Two common vowel patterns

Form I verbs are either **CaCaC** (katab, fata7, daras) or **CiCiC** (shirib, nizil, rikib). The endings are the same; only the stem vowels differ.

```ex
شرب / شربت | shirib / shiribt | he drank / I drank
نزل / نزلنا | nizil / nizilna | he went down / we went down
```

## Verbs that change shape

Verbs with **w** or **y** in the root change shape before the endings:

```ex
شاف / شفت | shaaf / shuft | he saw / I saw
نسي / نسيت | nisi / nisiit | he forgot / I forgot
حب / حبيت | 7abb / 7abbeet | he loved / I loved
```

See [verb classes](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/verb-classes) for why, and the [verb tables](/egyptian-arabic/conjugations) for full conjugations.

> To make it negative, wrap it in **ma-…-sh**: **ma katabtish** (I didn't write). See [saying "not"](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/negation).
