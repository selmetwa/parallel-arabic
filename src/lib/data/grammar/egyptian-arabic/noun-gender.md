Every noun in Arabic is either masculine or feminine. The gender matters because adjectives, verbs and words like "this" have to match it.

## Spotting a feminine noun

Most feminine nouns end in **ة** (taa marbuuta), which is said as **-a**.

```ex
عربية | 3arabiyya | car (f)
مدرسة | madrasa | school (f)
شقة | sha22a | flat (f)
```

Everything else is usually masculine: **beet** (house), **kitaab** (book), **baab** (door).

## Feminine nouns without ة

A few common words are feminine with no ending. Learn them as you meet them:

- female people: **umm** (mother), **bint** (girl), **sitt** (woman)
- body parts that come in pairs: **3een** (eye), **widn** (ear), **iid** (hand), **rigl** (leg)
- a few others: **shams** (sun), **ard** (ground), **naar** (fire)

## Making a feminine form

Words for people and adjectives add **-a** for the feminine.

```ex
مدرس / مدرسة | mudarris / mudarrisa | teacher (m / f)
مصري / مصرية | masri / masriyya | Egyptian (m / f)
تعبان / تعبانة | ta3baan / ta3baana | tired (m / f)
```

## The -a becomes -it

When a feminine noun is followed by an owner or a possessive ending, its **-a** is said **-it**.

```ex
عربية أحمد | 3arabiyyit a7mad | Ahmed's car
عربيتي | 3arabiyyiti | my car
```

## Why it matters

```ex
الولد ده طويل | il-walad da tawiil | This boy is tall.
البنت دي طويلة | il-bint di tawiila | This girl is tall.
```

> Plurals of *things* usually take feminine singular agreement: **il-3arabiyyaat di gdiida** (these cars are new). Plurals of *people* take plural agreement.
