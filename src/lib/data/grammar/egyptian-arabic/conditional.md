The main word for "if" in Egyptian is **law**. It covers both real conditions ("if it rains") and imagined ones ("if I were rich").

## Real conditions: law + past

After **law**, use the **past** tense, even when you're talking about the future. The result usually uses the future.

```ex
لو جت، هقولها | law gat, ha2ulha | If she comes, I'll tell her.
لو عندك وقت، تعالى | law 3andak wa2t, ta3aala | If you have time, come over.
لو ما جاش، كلمني | law ma gaash, kallimni | If he doesn't come, call me.
```

**iza** also means "if" and works the same way. It sounds a little more formal.

## Imagined conditions: law + kunt

To talk about something unreal, or something that didn't happen, put **kaan** (was) in both halves.

```ex
لو كنت أعرف، كنت قلتلك | law kunt a3raf, kunt 2ultilak | If I had known, I would have told you.
لو كان معايا فلوس، كنت اشتريتها | law kaan ma3aaya filuus, kunt ishtarett-ha | If I'd had money, I would have bought it.
```

**kaan** agrees with the person: **kunt** (I/you m), **kunti** (you f), **kaan** (he), **kaanit** (she), **kunna** (we), **kuntu** (you pl), **kaanu** (they).

## Useful combinations

```ex
حتى لو | 7atta law | even if
إلا لو | illa law | unless
لو سمحت | law sama7t | please, excuse me (literally "if you allow")
```

> **law sama7t** (to a man) and **law sama7ti** (to a woman) are the polite way to ask for something.
