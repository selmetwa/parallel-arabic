There are two ways to say "X's Y" or "the Y of X" in Egyptian: put the two nouns side by side, or use **bitaa3**.

## Two nouns side by side (iḍāfa)

Put the thing owned first, then the owner. The first noun **never** takes **il-** ("the"), even though the meaning is "the".

```ex
بيت أحمد | beet a7mad | Ahmed's house
باب البيت | baab il-beet | the door of the house
مفتاح العربية | muftaa7 il-3arabiyya | the car key
```

If the first noun is feminine, its **-a** becomes **-it**:

```ex
عربية أبويا | 3arabiyyit abuuya | my father's car
شنطة سارة | shantit saara | Sara's bag
```

## bitaa3: "belonging to"

**bitaa3** goes after the noun (which does take **il-**). It agrees with the thing owned:

| | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | بتاع bitaa3 | الكتاب بتاعي il-kitaab bitaa3i (my book) |
| feminine | بتاعة bitaa3it | العربية بتاعتي il-3arabiyya bita3ti (my car) |
| plural | بتوع bituu3 | الكتب بتوعي il-kutub bitu3i (my books) |

**bitaa3** takes the same endings as other nouns: **bitaa3ak** (your), **bitaa3ha** (her), and so on.

## When to use which

- Close, fixed relationships (family, parts of a whole) use the side-by-side form: **ukht 3ali** (Ali's sister).
- **bitaa3** is common with borrowed words, long phrases and everyday objects: **il-mubaayil bitaa3i** (my mobile).
- With a pronoun, both work: **kitaabi** and **il-kitaab bitaa3i** both mean "my book".

> **bitaa3** can also mean "the one for" or "the one of": **il-mudarris bitaa3 il-3arabi** (the Arabic teacher).
