If you've seen "I want 30 apple" in an example and wondered whether it was a mistake: it isn't. In Arabic, the form of the noun depends on the number in front of it, and from 11 upwards the noun is **singular**.

## The rule

| Number | Noun form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | singular (+ **waa7id** for emphasis) | كتاب kitaab |
| 2 | dual (**-een**) | كتابين kitabeen |
| 3 to 10 | plural | تلات كتب talat kutub |
| 11 and up | singular | حداشر كتاب 7idaashar kitaab |

So 3 to 10 is the only range that uses the plural. From 11 on, including 20, 100 and 1,000, the noun stays singular.

```ex
عايزة تلاتين تفاحة | 3ayza talatiin tuffaa7a | I (f) want thirty apples.
معاه مية جنيه | ma3aah miit gineeh | He has a hundred pounds.
عشرين سنة | 3ishriin sana | twenty years
```

## Numbers like 25: units first

Say the units first, then **w** ("and"), then the tens, as in the old English "five-and-twenty".

```ex
خمسة وعشرين | khamsa w 3ishriin | twenty-five
تلاتة وأربعين جنيه | talaata w arbi3iin gineeh | forty-three pounds
```

> "How many?" is **kaam**, and it also takes a singular noun: **kaam kitaab?** (how many books?).
