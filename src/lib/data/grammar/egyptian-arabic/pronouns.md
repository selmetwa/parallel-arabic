Egyptian Arabic has eight personal pronouns. English "you" splits into three: one for a man, one for a woman and one for a group.

## The pronouns

| English | Egyptian | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | أنا | ana |
| you (to a man) | إنتَ | inta |
| you (to a woman) | إنتِ | inti |
| he | هوَّ | huwwa |
| she | هيَّ | hiyya |
| we | إحنا | i7na |
| you (to a group) | إنتوا | intu |
| they | هُمَّ | humma |

There is no separate feminine "you all" or "they", as there is in Modern Standard Arabic. Use **intu** and **humma** for any group.

## No verb "to be" in the present

To say "I am…" or "she is…", put the pronoun next to the description. Nothing goes in between.

```ex
أنا مِن مَصر | ana min masr | I'm from Egypt.
إنتِ تَعبانة؟ | inti ta3baana? | Are you (f) tired?
هُمَّ في البيت | humma fil-beet | They're at home.
```

## You can usually drop the pronoun

A verb already shows who is doing it (see [the present tense](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/present-tense)), so the pronoun is optional. Adding it gives emphasis, like stressing "*I* do" in English.

```ex
بحب القهوة | ba7ebb il-2ahwa | I love coffee.
أنا بحب القهوة، هوَّ بيحب الشاي | ana ba7ebb il-2ahwa, huwwa bi7ebb ish-shaay | *I* love coffee; *he* loves tea.
```

> The pronoun you choose for "you" decides the form of the verb and adjective that follow, so pay attention to who you're talking to: **inta 3aamil eeh?** to a man, **inti 3amla eeh?** to a woman ("how are you doing?").
