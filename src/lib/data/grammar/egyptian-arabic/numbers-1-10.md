Egyptian numbers have two forms: one for counting out loud, and a shorter one used right before a noun.

## The numbers

| | Egyptian | Counting | Before a noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | صفر | sifr | |
| 1 | واحد | waa7id | (see below) |
| 2 | اتنين | itneen | (see below) |
| 3 | تلاتة | talaata | talat |
| 4 | أربعة | arba3a | arba3 |
| 5 | خمسة | khamsa | khamas |
| 6 | ستة | sitta | sitt |
| 7 | سبعة | sab3a | saba3 |
| 8 | تمانية | tamanya | taman |
| 9 | تسعة | tis3a | tisa3 |
| 10 | عشرة | 3ashara | 3ashar |

The short form drops the final **-a**: **talat kutub** (three books), **khamas da2aayi2** (five minutes).

## One and two

You don't say "one" or "two" in front of a noun.

- **One**: use the noun on its own. Add **waa7id** (or **waa7da** for feminine) *after* it for emphasis.
- **Two**: use the [dual](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/dual) ending **-een**.

```ex
عايز كتاب واحد | 3aayiz kitaab waa7id | I want one book.
عندي أختين | 3andi ukhteen | I have two sisters.
```

## Three to ten take the plural

```ex
تلات كتب | talat kutub | three books
سبع سنين | saba3 siniin | seven years
```

A few plurals beginning with a vowel take an extra **-t-** after the number: **talattiyyaam** (three days), **arba3tushhur** (four months), **khamastalaaf** (five thousand).

## Writing the numbers

Egypt mostly uses Eastern Arabic digits, written left to right like Western ones: ٠ ١ ٢ ٣ ٤ ٥ ٦ ٧ ٨ ٩.

> From 11 upwards the noun goes back to the **singular**. See [counting things](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/numbers-counting).
