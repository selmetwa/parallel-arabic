Most Arabic words grow from a **root** of three consonants that carries a core meaning. A verb **form** is a fixed pattern laid over the root, and each pattern nudges the meaning in a predictable direction. Once you know the patterns, you can often guess what a new verb means and how it conjugates.

## Roots

The root **k-t-b** is about writing:

```ex
كتب | katab | he wrote
كتاب | kitaab | book
مكتب | maktab | office, desk
مكتوب | maktuub | written
```

## The forms Egyptian uses most

Grammarians number the patterns with Roman numerals and use **f-3-l** as a placeholder root.

| Form | Pattern | Present | Typical meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | fa3al / fi3il | yif3al | the basic meaning | katab (write), shirib (drink) |
| II | fa33al | yifa33al | make someone do it; intensive | darris (teach), ghayyar (change) |
| III | faa3il | yifaa3il | do it with/to someone | saa3id (help), 2aabil (meet) |
| V | itfa33al | yitfa33al | reflexive of II | it3allim (learn), it7arrak (move) |
| VI | itfaa3il | yitfaa3il | do it to each other | itkhaani2 (argue with each other) |
| VII | infa3al | yinfi3il | get done | inbasat (be pleased) |
| VIII | ifta3al | yifti3il | self-directed | ishtaghal (work), i7taram (respect) |
| X | istaf3al | yistaf3il | seek, consider | ista3mil (use), istanna (wait) |

Form IV (**af3al**) belongs mainly to Modern Standard Arabic. Egyptian usually uses Form II or I instead.

## Pairs to spot

The forms make families of related verbs from one root:

```ex
علم ← اتعلم | 3allim → it3allim | teach → learn (II → V)
درس ← درّس | daras → darris | study → teach (I → II)
فتح ← اتفتح | fata7 → itfata7 | open → be opened
```

## Egyptian's it- passive

Egyptian makes passives of Form I with **it-**: **itfata7** (was opened), **itkatab** (was written), **itsara2** (was stolen). This replaces Form VII for many verbs.
