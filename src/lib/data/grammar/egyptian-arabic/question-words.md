Egyptian question words often sit **where the answer would go**, which is usually at the end of the sentence. That's different from English, where they jump to the front.

## The question words

| English | Egyptian | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| what | إيه | eeh |
| where | فين | feen |
| when | إمتى | imta |
| why | ليه | leeh |
| how | إزاي | izzaay |
| who | مين | miin |
| how many / how much | كام | kaam |
| how much (price) | بكام | bikaam |
| which | أنهي | anhi |

## Word order

```ex
إنت بتعمل إيه؟ | inta bti3mil eeh? | What are you (m) doing?
إنتي ساكنة فين؟ | inti sakna feen? | Where do you (f) live?
الحفلة إمتى؟ | il-7afla imta? | When is the party?
ده مين؟ | da miin? | Who's that?
```

**leeh**, **izzaay** and **imta** can also go at the start:

```ex
ليه اتأخرت؟ | leeh it2akhkhart? | Why were you late?
إزايك؟ | izzayyak? | How are you (m)?
```

## kaam + singular

**kaam** takes a singular noun, just like numbers from 11 up.

```ex
عندك كام أخ؟ | 3andak kaam akh? | How many brothers do you have?
الساعة كام؟ | is-saa3a kaam? | What time is it?
ده بكام؟ | da bikaam? | How much is this?
```

## Yes/no questions

Just raise your voice at the end. No word order change is needed.

```ex
إنت مصري؟ | inta masri? | Are you Egyptian?
```

> **feen** combines with prepositions: **min feen?** (from where?), **3ala feen?** (where to?).
