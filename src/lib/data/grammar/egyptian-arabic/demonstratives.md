Egyptian uses one small set of words for both "this" and "that". The tricky part is where they go: **after** the noun.

## The forms

| | Egyptian | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| this/that (masculine) | ده | da |
| this/that (feminine) | دي | di |
| these/those (plural) | دول | dool |

## After the noun: "this house"

When you mean "this book" or "that girl", the noun takes **il-** ("the") and the demonstrative comes after it.

```ex
الكتاب ده حلو | il-kitaab da 7ilw | This book is nice.
البنت دي أختي | il-bint di ukhti | This girl is my sister.
الناس دول جيرانّا | in-naas dool giranna | These people are our neighbours.
```

## Before the noun: "this is a house"

Put the demonstrative first and leave the noun without **il-**, and you've made a whole sentence ("this is…").

```ex
ده كتابي | da kitaabi | This is my book.
دي عربية جديدة | di 3arabiyya gdiida | This is a new car.
```

Compare **il-beet da** (this house) with **da beet** (this is a house).

## "That" when you need it

There are longer forms for "that one over there": **dukha** (m), **dikha** (f), **dukham** (pl). Day to day, most people just use **da**, **di** and **dool** and point.

> Use the plural **dool** for groups of people. For a group of things, **di** (feminine singular) is very common: **il-kutub di** (these books).
