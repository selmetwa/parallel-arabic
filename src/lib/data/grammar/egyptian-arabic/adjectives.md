Adjectives come **after** the noun and match it in gender, number and "the".

## Matching gender

```ex
ولد كبير | walad kibiir | a big boy
بنت كبيرة | bint kibiira | a big girl
```

## Matching "the": description vs sentence

If the noun has **il-**, the adjective takes **il-** too. Leave it off the adjective and you've made a sentence instead.

```ex
الولد الكبير | il-walad il-kibiir | the big boy
الولد كبير | il-walad kibiir | The boy is big.
```

## Matching number

People take plural adjectives. Many adjectives have broken plurals:

```ex
ولاد كبار | wilaad kubaar | big boys
ناس طيبين | naas tayyibiin | kind people
```

Plurals of things usually take the feminine singular:

```ex
عربيات جديدة | 3arabiyyaat gdiida | new cars
```

## Nationality adjectives (-i)

Add **-i** to a place name; the feminine is **-iyya**, the plural **-iyyiin**.

```ex
مصري / مصرية / مصريين | masri / masriyya / masriyyiin | Egyptian (m / f / pl)
```

## Colours

Colours have their own pattern:

| Masculine | Feminine | Plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| أحمر a7mar | حمرا 7amra | حمر 7umr | red |
| أزرق azra2 | زرقا zar2a | زرق zur2 | blue |
| أبيض abyad | بيضا beeda | بيض biid | white |
| إسود iswid | سودا sooda | سود suud | black |

> Adjectives also have a comparative form: **kibiir** (big) → **akbar** (bigger, biggest). See [comparing](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/comparatives).
