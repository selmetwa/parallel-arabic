Arabic says "my", "your", "his" and so on with short endings attached to the noun. The same endings also mark the object of a verb ("saw *me*") and follow prepositions ("with *me*"), so they're worth learning early.

## Possessive endings

| | Ending | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | ـي -i | كتابي kitaabi | my book |
| your (m) | ـك -ak | كتابك kitaabak | your book |
| your (f) | ـك -ik | كتابك kitaabik | your book |
| his | ـه -u | كتابه kitaabu | his book |
| her | ـها -ha | كتابها kitabha | her book |
| our | ـنا -na | كتابنا kitabna | our book |
| your (pl) | ـكو -ku | كتابكو kitabku | your book |
| their | ـهم -hum | كتابهم kitabhum | their book |

Some things to notice:

- **-ak** and **-ik** are both written **ـك**. Only the vowel tells you whether it's a man or a woman.
- **ـه** is said **-u**, not "h".
- After a vowel, "my" is **-ya**: **abuuya** (my father), **ma3aaya** (with me).
- A long vowel shortens when an ending makes the word longer: **kitaab** becomes **kitabna**.
- Feminine **-a** becomes **-it**: **3arabiyya** becomes **3arabiyyiti** (my car).

## The same endings as objects

After a verb, the endings mean "me, you, him…". The one difference is "me", which is **-ni**.

```ex
شافني | shafni | he saw me
شافك | shafak | he saw you (m)
شافها | shafha | he saw her
شافهم | shafhum | he saw them
```

## After prepositions

```ex
معايا | ma3aaya | with me
ليك | liik | for you (m)
عليه | 3alee | on him
منها | minha | from her
```

## "To have" is a preposition

Egyptian has no verb "to have". Instead it uses **3and** ("at, with") plus an ending.

```ex
عندي عربية | 3andi 3arabiyya | I have a car.
عندك وقت؟ | 3andak wa2t? | Do you (m) have time?
معندناش فلوس | ma3andinaash filuus | We don't have money.
```

> See [possession](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/possession) for **bitaa3**, the other common way to say "my".
