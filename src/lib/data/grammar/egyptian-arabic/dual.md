Arabic has a special form for exactly two of something: the dual. In Egyptian it's the ending **-een**.

## Making the dual

Add **-een** to the singular.

```ex
كتاب ← كتابين | kitaab → kitabeen | a book → two books
يوم ← يومين | yoom → yomeen | a day → two days
```

Feminine nouns ending in **-a** use **-teen**:

```ex
عربية ← عربيتين | 3arabiyya → 3arabiyteen | a car → two cars
سنة ← سنتين | sana → santeen | a year → two years
مرة ← مرتين | marra → marrateen | once → twice
```

## Don't add the number 2

The dual already means "two", so you don't say *itneen kitaab*. If you want to stress "two", put **itneen** after the noun:

```ex
عايز كتابين | 3aayiz kitabeen | I want two books.
عايز كتابين اتنين بس | 3aayiz kitabeen itneen bass | I want just two books.
```

## Words that go with a dual

Adjectives and demonstratives after a dual noun are plural.

```ex
الولدين دول | il-waladeen dool | these two boys
```

> Pairs of body parts have a dual too: **3eneen** (two eyes, eyes), **ideen** (hands). See [numbers 1 to 10](/grammar/egyptian-arabic/numbers-1-10) for how the dual fits with counting.
