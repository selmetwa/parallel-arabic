Arabic uses one pattern, **af3al**, for both "bigger" and "the biggest". It doesn't change for gender or number.

## Making the comparative

Take the three root letters of the adjective and put them into **a_ _a_**:

| Adjective | Comparative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كبير kibiir | أكبر akbar | bigger |
| صغير sughayyar | أصغر asghar | smaller |
| حلو 7ilw | أحلى a7la | nicer, sweeter |
| كويس kwayyis | أحسن a7san | better |
| غالي ghaali | أغلى aghla | more expensive |
| رخيص rikhiis | أرخص arkhas | cheaper |
| بعيد bi3iid | أبعد ab3ad | further |

**kwayyis** (good) takes **a7san**, from a different root.

## "Than" is min

```ex
القاهرة أكبر من إسكندرية | il-qaahira akbar min iskindiriyya | Cairo is bigger than Alexandria.
هي أطول منه | hiyya atwal minnu | She's taller than him.
ده أرخص من ده | da arkhas min da | This one is cheaper than that one.
```

The comparative doesn't change for **hiyya**: **hiyya akbar**, not *kubra*.

## The superlative: before the noun

Put the comparative **before** a noun to mean "the most":

```ex
أكبر بيت | akbar beet | the biggest house
أحسن مطعم في البلد | a7san mat3am fil-balad | the best restaurant in town
```

Or use it on its own with **il-**:

```ex
هو الأحسن | huwwa il-a7san | He's the best.
```

## Adjectives without the pattern

Longer adjectives and participles use **aktar** (more) after the adjective:

```ex
مبسوط أكتر | mabsuut aktar | happier
مشغولة أكتر مني | mashghuula aktar minni | busier than me (f)
```
