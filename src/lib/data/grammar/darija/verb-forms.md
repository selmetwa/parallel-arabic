Most Arabic words grow from a **root** of three consonants that carries a core meaning. A verb **form** is a fixed pattern laid over the root, and each pattern nudges the meaning in a predictable direction.

## Roots

The root **k-t-b** is about writing:

```ex
كتب | kteb | he wrote
كتاب | ktab | book
مكتب | mekteb | office, desk
مكتوب | mektub | written; fate
```

## The forms Darija uses most

Grammarians number the patterns with Roman numerals and use **f-3-l** as a placeholder root. Darija drops many short vowels, so the patterns look more compressed than in other dialects.

| Form | Pattern | Typical meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | f3el | the basic meaning | kteb (write), shreb (drink) |
| II | fe33el | make someone do it; intensive | 3llem (teach), beddel (change) |
| III | fa3el | do it with/to someone | 3awen (help), sameh (forgive) |
| V | tfe33el | reflexive of II | t3llem (learn), t7errek (move) |
| VI | tfa3el | do it to each other | tlaqaw (they met) |
| VIII | fta3el | self-directed | khtar (choose), 7tarem (respect) |
| X | stef3el | seek, consider | sta3mel (use) |

Form IV (**af3al**) belongs to Modern Standard Arabic. Darija uses Form II or I instead.

## Pairs to spot

```ex
علّم ← تعلّم | 3llem → t3llem | teach → learn (II → V)
حرّك ← تحرّك | 7errek → t7errek | move something → move yourself
```

## Darija's t- passive

Darija makes passives with **t-** or **tt-**: **ttkteb** (was written), **t7ell** (was opened), **ttkla** (was eaten).
