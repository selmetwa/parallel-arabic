Arabic says "my", "your", "his" and so on with short endings attached to the noun. The same endings also mark the object of a verb ("saw *me*") and follow prepositions ("with *me*").

## Possessive endings

| | Ending | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | ـي -i | كتابي ktabi | my book |
| your (m or f) | ـك -ek | كتابك ktabek | your book |
| his | ـو -u | كتابو ktabu | his book |
| her | ـها -ha | كتابها ktabha | her book |
| our | ـنا -na | كتابنا ktabna | our book |
| your (pl) | ـكم -kum | كتابكم ktabkum | your book |
| their | ـهم -hum | كتابهم ktabhum | their book |

Darija usually has one "your" ending, **-ek**, for both a man and a woman. After a vowel, "my" is **-ya** (**khaya**, my brother) and "his" is **-h** (**khah**, his brother).

## dyal: "of" for everything

In everyday speech, Darija very often uses **dyal** ("belonging to") instead of an ending, especially with longer or borrowed words. **dyal** never changes for gender or number:

```ex
الكتاب ديالي | l-ktab dyali | my book
الطوموبيل ديالك | t-tomobil dyalek | your car
الولاد ديالهم | l-wlad dyalhum | their children
```

## The same endings as objects

After a verb, the endings mean "me, you, him…". "Me" is **-ni**.

```ex
شافني | shafni | he saw me
شافك | shafek | he saw you
شافها | shafha | he saw her
شافهم | shafhum | he saw them
```

## After prepositions

```ex
معايا | m3aya | with me
ليك | lik | for you
عليه | 3lih | on him
منها | menha | from her
```

## "To have" is a preposition

Darija has no verb "to have". It uses **3nd** ("at") plus an ending:

```ex
عندي طوموبيل | 3ndi tomobil | I have a car.
عندك الوقت؟ | 3ndek l-weqt? | Do you have time?
ما عندناش الفلوس | ma 3ndnash l-flus | We don't have money.
```
