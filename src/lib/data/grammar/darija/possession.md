There are two ways to say "X's Y" in Darija: put the two nouns side by side, or link them with **dyal** ("of, belonging to"). Darija uses **dyal** much more than other dialects use their equivalents.

## dyal: the everyday way

Put the thing owned first with **l-**, then **dyal**, then the owner:

```ex
الدار ديال أحمد | d-dar dyal a7med | Ahmed's house
الساروت ديال الطوموبيل | s-sarut dyal t-tomobil | the car key
```

**dyal** never changes: the same word works for masculine, feminine and plural.

With an ending, **dyal** means "my, your, his…": **dyali**, **dyalek**, **dyalu**, **dyalha**, **dyalna**, **dyalkum**, **dyalhum**.

```ex
هاد التيليفون ديالي | had t-tilifun dyali | This phone is mine.
ديال من هاد الشكارة؟ | dyal men had sh-shkara? | Whose bag is this?
```

## Two nouns side by side

For family, parts of the body and fixed expressions, put the two nouns together. The first noun has no **l-**:

```ex
دار خويا | dar khuya | my brother's house
باب الدار | bab d-dar | the door of the house
```

If the first noun ends in **-a**, it becomes **-t**: **mrat a7med** (Ahmed's wife).

> When in doubt, **dyal** is always understood: **l-ktab dyal l-ostad** (the teacher's book).
