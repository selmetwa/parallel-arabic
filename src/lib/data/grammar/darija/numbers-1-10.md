The Darija numbers are close to the other dialects', with one big difference: "two" is **jouj**.

## The numbers

| | Darija | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | صفر | sifr |
| 1 | واحد | wa7ed |
| 2 | جوج | jouj |
| 3 | تلاتة | tlata |
| 4 | ربعة | reb3a |
| 5 | خمسة | khamsa |
| 6 | ستة | setta |
| 7 | سبعة | seb3a |
| 8 | تمنية | tmenya |
| 9 | تسعود | tes3ud |
| 10 | عشرة | 3eshra |

## One: wa7ed l-

**wa7ed** + a noun with **l-** means "a" or "one":

```ex
واحد الراجل | wa7ed r-rajel | a man, one man
واحد الدار | wa7ed d-dar | a house
```

## Two: jouj

**jouj** + the plural, or the dual ending **-ayn** for time words (see [the dual](/grammar/darija/dual)):

```ex
جوج كتوب | jouj ktub | two books
يومين | yumayn | two days
```

## Three to ten take the plural

From 3 to 10, use the number, often **d**, then the plural with **l-**:

```ex
تلاتة د الولاد | tlata d l-wlad | three children
خمسة د الكتوب | khamsa d l-ktub | five books
```

With some common time words, a short form is used instead: **tlat iyyam** (three days), **khmes snin** (five years).

> From 11 upwards the noun goes back to the **singular**. See [counting things](/grammar/darija/numbers-counting).
