A Darija present-tense verb has three parts: a **person prefix** that shows who is doing it, the **verb stem**, and usually **ka-** in front for the normal present.

## The bare present and the ka- present

Here is **kteb** (to write), stem **-ktb**:

| Person | Bare form | With ka- |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | nkteb | kanekteb |
| nta (you m) | tkteb | katekteb |
| nti (you f) | tketbi | katketbi |
| huwa (he) | ykteb | kayekteb |
| hiya (she) | tkteb | katekteb |
| 7na (we) | nketbu | kanketbu |
| ntuma (you pl) | tketbu | katketbu |
| huma (they) | yketbu | kayketbu |

Unlike other dialects, "I" starts with **n-**, and "we" is **n-** plus **-u**.

## ka-: the normal present

```ex
كنخدم فالبنك | kankhdem f l-bank | I work in a bank.
كتهضر العربية؟ | kathder l-3arabiya? | Do you speak Arabic?
```

**ka-** covers both "I do" and "I'm doing". Some regions say **ta-** instead: **tankhdem**.

## Drop ka- after "want", "must", "can"

Use the bare form after these. English uses "to" + verb here; Darija uses the bare present with the right person.

```ex
بغيت نكتب | bghit nkteb | I want to write.
خصك تجي | khassek tji | You have to come.
نقدر نعاونك؟ | nqder n3awnek? | Can I help you?
```

**khass-** ("must") takes an ending for the person: **khassni** (I must), **khassha** (she must).

> For "will", put **ghadi** in front of the bare form. See [the future](/grammar/darija/future).
