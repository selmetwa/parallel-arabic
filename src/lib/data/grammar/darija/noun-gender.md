Every noun in Arabic is either masculine or feminine. The gender matters because adjectives, verbs and words like "this" have to match it.

## Spotting a feminine noun

Most feminine nouns end in **ة**, said **-a**:

```ex
مدينة | mdina | city (f)
طابلة | tabla | table (f)
خدمة | khedma | work, job (f)
```

## Feminine nouns without ة

Some very common words are feminine with no ending:

- **dar** (house), **tomobil** (car), **triq** (road)
- female people: **umm** / **mmi** (mother), **bent** (girl)
- paired body parts: **3in** (eye), **yedd** (hand), **rijl** (leg)
- **shems** (sun), **ard** (ground)

## Making a feminine form

People and adjectives add **-a**:

```ex
أستاذ / أستاذة | ostad / ostada | teacher (m / f)
مغربي / مغربية | meghribi / meghribiya | Moroccan (m / f)
عيان / عيانة | 3eyyan / 3eyyana | tired (m / f)
```

## The -a becomes -t

Before a possessive ending, the feminine **-a** becomes **-t**:

```ex
خدمتي | khdemti | my work
مدينتنا | mdinetna | our city
```

## Why it matters

```ex
هاد الولد طويل | had l-weld twil | This boy is tall.
هاد البنت طويلة | had l-bent twila | This girl is tall.
```

> Many French loanwords keep a gender of their own: **tomobil** (car) is feminine, **telefon** (phone) is masculine.
