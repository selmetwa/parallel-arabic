The past tense is the simplest Arabic tense: one stem, with an ending for each person.

## Endings

Here is **kteb** (he wrote):

| Person | Ending | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | -t | ktebt |
| nta (you m) | -ti | ktebti |
| nti (you f) | -ti | ktebti |
| huwa (he) | (none) | kteb |
| hiya (she) | -at | ketbat |
| 7na (we) | -na | ktebna |
| ntuma (you pl) | -tiw | ktebtiw |
| huma (they) | -u | ketbu |

"You (m)" and "you (f)" are the same in the past. "He" has no ending, so it's the form you'll find in the verb tables.

```ex
كتبت البرا البارح | ktebt l-bra l-bare7 | I wrote the letter yesterday.
هي خرجات بكري | hiya khrjat bekri | She went out early.
شربنا أتاي | shrebna atay | We drank tea.
```

## Verbs that change shape

Verbs with **w** or **y** in the root change shape before the endings:

```ex
شاف / شفت | shaf / shft | he saw / I saw
نسا / نسيت | nsa / nsit | he forgot / I forgot
حل / حليت | 7ell / 7llit | he opened / I opened
```

See [verb classes](/grammar/darija/verb-classes) for why, and the [verb tables](/darija/conjugations) for full conjugations.

> To make it negative, wrap it in **ma-…-sh**: **ma ktebtsh** (I didn't write). See [saying "not"](/grammar/darija/negation).
