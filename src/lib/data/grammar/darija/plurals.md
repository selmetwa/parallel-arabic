Arabic makes plurals in two ways: by adding an ending (a "sound" plural), or by changing the vowels inside the word (a "broken" plural).

## Sound plurals: add an ending

**-in** is mostly for male people and occupations:

```ex
خدام ← خدامين | khddam → khddamin | worker → workers
```

**-at** is for most feminine nouns, and many borrowed words:

```ex
طابلة ← طابلات | tabla → tablat | table → tables
طوموبيل ← طوموبيلات | tomobil → tomobilat | car → cars
```

## Broken plurals: the word changes shape

| Singular | Plural | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كتاب ktab | كتوب ktub | book |
| دار dar | ديور dyur | house |
| ولد weld | ولاد wlad | boy, child |
| بنت bent | بنات bnat | girl |
| راجل rajel | رجال rjal | man |
| كلب kelb | كلاب klab | dog |
| شرجم sherjem | شراجم shrajem | window |

There's no reliable way to predict a broken plural. Learn each one with its singular.

## Collective nouns

Many foods and plants have a "collective" form. Add **-a** for one item:

```ex
تفاح | teffa7 | apples (in general)
تفاحة | teffa7a | one apple
```

> Numbers change which form you use: 3 to 10 take the plural, but 11 and up go back to the singular. See [counting things](/grammar/darija/numbers-counting).
