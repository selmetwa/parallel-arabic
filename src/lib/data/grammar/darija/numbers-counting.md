If you've seen "I want 30 apple" in an example and wondered whether it was a mistake: it isn't. In Arabic, the form of the noun depends on the number in front of it, and from 11 upwards the noun is **singular**.

## The rule

| Number | Noun form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **wa7ed l-** + singular | واحد الكتاب wa7ed l-ktab |
| 2 | **jouj** + plural (or **-ayn**) | جوج كتوب jouj ktub |
| 3 to 10 | plural | تلاتة د الكتوب tlata d l-ktub |
| 11 and up | singular | عشرين كتاب 3eshrin ktab |

So 3 to 10 is the range that uses the plural. From 11 on, including 20, 100 and 1,000, the noun stays singular.

```ex
بغيت تلاتين تفاحة | bghit tlatin teffa7a | I want thirty apples.
عندو مية درهم | 3ndu mya derhem | He has a hundred dirhams.
عشرين عام | 3eshrin 3am | twenty years
```

## Numbers like 25: units first

```ex
خمسة وعشرين | khamsa w 3eshrin | twenty-five
```

> Prices are often given in **ryal** in speech (1 dirham = 20 ryal), so **mya ryal** is 5 dirhams. Ask **b-sh7al?** (how much?) and listen for which one is meant.
