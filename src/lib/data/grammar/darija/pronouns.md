Moroccan Darija has eight personal pronouns. English "you" splits into three: one for a man, one for a woman and one for a group.

## The pronouns

| English | Darija | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| I | أنا | ana |
| you (to a man) | نتا | nta |
| you (to a woman) | نتي | nti |
| he | هو | huwa |
| she | هي | hiya |
| we | حنا | 7na |
| you (to a group) | نتوما | ntuma |
| they | هوما | huma |

## No verb "to be" in the present

Put the pronoun next to the description. Nothing goes in between.

```ex
أنا من المغرب | ana men l-meghrib | I'm from Morocco.
نتي عيانة؟ | nti 3eyyana? | Are you (f) tired?
هوما فالدار | huma f-dar | They're at home.
```

## You can usually drop the pronoun

The verb already shows who is doing it, so the pronoun is optional. Adding it gives emphasis.

```ex
كنبغي القهوة | kanbghi l-qehwa | I love coffee.
أنا كنبغي القهوة، هو كيبغي أتاي | ana kanbghi l-qehwa, huwa kaybghi atay | *I* love coffee; *he* loves tea.
```

> In the past tense, Darija often uses the same form for "you (m)" and "you (f)": **ktebti** (you wrote). The pronoun **nta** / **nti** makes it clear when needed.
