Levantine question words usually go at the **start** of the question, as in English.

## The question words

| English | Levantine | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| what | شو | shu |
| where | وين | wein |
| when | إيمتى | eemta |
| why | ليش | leish |
| how | كيف | kiif |
| who | مين | miin |
| how many / how much | قدّيش | 2addeish |
| how many | كم | kam |
| which | أيّ | ayy |

## Examples

```ex
شو عم تعمل؟ | shu 3am ta3mel? | What are you (m) doing?
وين ساكنة؟ | wein saakne? | Where do you (f) live?
إيمتى الحفلة؟ | eemta il-7afle? | When is the party?
ليش تأخرت؟ | leish t2akhkhart? | Why were you late?
كيفك؟ | kiifak? | How are you (m)?
مين هادا؟ | miin haada? | Who's that?
```

## How many, how much

**kam** and **2addeish** take a singular noun:

```ex
كم ولد عندك؟ | kam walad 3andak? | How many children do you have?
قدّيش الساعة؟ | 2addeish is-saa3a? | What time is it?
بقدّيش هادا؟ | b-2addeish haada? | How much is this?
```

## Yes/no questions

Raise your voice at the end. No word order change is needed.

```ex
إنت سوري؟ | inta suuri? | Are you Syrian?
```

> **wein** combines with prepositions: **min wein?** (from where?), **la-wein?** (where to?).
