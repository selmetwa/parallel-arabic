Levantine numbers have two forms: one for counting out loud, and a shorter one used right before a noun.

## The numbers

| | Levantine | Counting | Before a noun |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | صفر | sifr | |
| 1 | واحد | waa7ad | (see below) |
| 2 | تنين | tnein | (see below) |
| 3 | تلاتة | tlaate | tlat |
| 4 | أربعة | arba3a | arba3 |
| 5 | خمسة | khamse | khams |
| 6 | ستة | sitte | sitt |
| 7 | سبعة | sab3a | sab3 |
| 8 | تمانية | tmaanye | tman |
| 9 | تسعة | tis3a | tis3 |
| 10 | عشرة | 3ashra | 3ashr |

The short form drops the final vowel: **tlat kutub** (three books), **khams da2aaye2** (five minutes).

## One and two

You don't usually say "one" or "two" in front of a noun.

- **One**: use the noun on its own. Add **waa7ad** (or **wa7de** for feminine) *after* it for emphasis.
- **Two**: use the [dual](/grammar/levantine/dual) ending **-ein**.

```ex
بدي كتاب واحد | baddi ktaab waa7ad | I want one book.
عندي أختين | 3andi ukhtein | I have two sisters.
```

## Three to ten take the plural

```ex
تلات كتب | tlat kutub | three books
سبع سنين | sab3 sniin | seven years
```

A few plurals beginning with a vowel take an extra **-t-**: **tlatt iyyaam** (three days), **arba3t ashhur** (four months), **khamst alaaf** (five thousand).

> From 11 upwards the noun goes back to the **singular**. See [counting things](/grammar/levantine/numbers-counting).
