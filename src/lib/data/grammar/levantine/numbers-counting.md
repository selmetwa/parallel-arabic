If you've seen "I want 30 apple" in an example and wondered whether it was a mistake: it isn't. In Arabic, the form of the noun depends on the number in front of it, and from 11 upwards the noun is **singular**.

## The rule

| Number | Noun form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | singular (+ **waa7ad** for emphasis) | كتاب ktaab |
| 2 | dual (**-ein**) | كتابين ktaabein |
| 3 to 10 | plural | تلات كتب tlat kutub |
| 11 and up | singular | عشرين كتاب 3ishriin ktaab |

So 3 to 10 is the only range that uses the plural. From 11 on, including 20, 100 and 1,000, the noun stays singular.

```ex
بدي تلاتين تفّاحة | baddi tlaatiin tiffaa7a | I want thirty apples.
معه مية دولار | ma3o miit dolaar | He has a hundred dollars.
عشرين سنة | 3ishriin sine | twenty years
```

## 11 to 19

| Number | Levantine | Number | Levantine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 11 | حدعش 7da3sh | 16 | ستعش sitta3sh |
| 12 | تنعش tna3sh | 17 | سبعتعش saba3ta3sh |
| 13 | تلتعش tlatta3sh | 18 | تمنتعش tmanta3sh |
| 14 | أربعتعش arba3ta3sh | 19 | تسعتعش tis3ta3sh |
| 15 | خمستعش khamsta3sh | | |

Before a noun, these usually add **-ar**: **tna3shar sine** (twelve years).

## Tens, hundreds, thousands

| Number | Levantine | Number | Levantine |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | عشرين 3ishriin | 60 | ستين sittiin |
| 30 | تلاتين tlaatiin | 70 | سبعين sab3iin |
| 40 | أربعين arb3iin | 80 | تمانين tmaaniin |
| 50 | خمسين khamsiin | 90 | تسعين tis3iin |
| 100 | مية miyye (مِيت miit before a noun) | 1,000 | ألف alf |

## Numbers like 25: units first

Say the units first, then **w** ("and"), then the tens:

```ex
خمسة وعشرين | khamse w 3ishriin | twenty-five
```

> "How many?" is **2addeish**, and it takes a singular noun: **2addeish walad 3andak?** (how many children do you have?).
