Every noun in Arabic is either masculine or feminine. The gender matters because adjectives, verbs and words like "this" have to match it.

## Spotting a feminine noun

Most feminine nouns end in **ة** (taa marbuuta). In Levantine it's said **-a** after some consonants and **-e** after others.

```ex
سيارة | sayyaara | car (f)
مدرسة | madrase | school (f)
غرفة | ghurfe | room (f)
```

Everything else is usually masculine: **beit** (house), **ktaab** (book), **baab** (door).

## Feminine nouns without ة

A few common words are feminine with no ending:

- female people: **imm** (mother), **binit** (girl)
- paired body parts: **3ein** (eye), **2iid** (hand), **ijir** (leg)
- a few others: **shams** (sun), **ard** (ground)

## Making a feminine form

People and adjectives add **-a/-e**:

```ex
أستاذ / أستاذة | istaaz / istaaze | teacher (m / f)
سوري / سورية | suuri / suuriyye | Syrian (m / f)
تعبان / تعبانة | ta3baan / ta3baane | tired (m / f)
```

## The -a/-e becomes -it

Before an owner or a possessive ending, the feminine ending is said **-it**:

```ex
سيارة أحمد | sayyaarit a7mad | Ahmed's car
سيارتي | sayyaarti | my car
```

## Why it matters

```ex
هالولد طويل | hal-walad tawiil | This boy is tall.
هالبنت طويلة | hal-binit tawiile | This girl is tall.
```

> Plurals of *things* usually take feminine singular agreement: **is-sayyaaraat jdiide** (the cars are new).
