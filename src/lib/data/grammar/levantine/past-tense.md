The past tense is the simplest Arabic tense: one stem, with an ending for each person.

## Endings

Here is **katab** (he wrote):

| Person | Ending | Form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | -t | katabt |
| inta (you m) | -t | katabt |
| inti (you f) | -ti | katabti |
| huwwe (he) | (none) | katab |
| hiyye (she) | -it | katbit |
| ni7na (we) | -na | katabna |
| intu (you pl) | -tu | katabtu |
| hinne (they) | -u | katabu |

"He" has no ending, so it's the form you'll find in dictionaries and in the verb tables.

```ex
كتبت الرسالة مبارح | katabt ir-risaale mbaare7 | I wrote the letter yesterday.
هيّي طلعت بكير | hiyye tal3it bakkiir | She went out early.
شربنا شاي | sharabna shaay | We drank tea.
```

## Verbs that change shape

Verbs with **w** or **y** in the root change shape before the endings:

```ex
شاف / شفت | shaaf / shift | he saw / I saw
نسي / نسيت | nisi / nsiit | he forgot / I forgot
حبّ / حبّيت | 7abb / 7abbeit | he loved / I loved
```

See [verb classes](/grammar/levantine/verb-classes) for why, and the [verb tables](/levantine/conjugations) for full conjugations.

> To make it negative, put **ma** in front: **ma katabt** (I didn't write). See [saying "not"](/grammar/levantine/negation).
