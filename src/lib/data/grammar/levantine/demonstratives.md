Levantine has a handy short form, **hal-**, that means "this" or "these" and attaches straight to the noun. There are also full words for "this" and "that".

## hal-: the everyday "this"

**hal-** replaces **il-** ("the") and works for any gender or number.

```ex
هالبيت كبير | hal-beit kbiir | This house is big.
هالبنت أختي | hal-binit ukhti | This girl is my sister.
هالأيام | hal-iyyaam | these days
```

## The full forms

| | This | That |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | هادا haada | هداك hadaak |
| feminine | هاي haay (هادي haadi) | هديك hadiik |
| plural | هدول hadool | هدولاك hadolaak |

## "This is…"

Put the demonstrative first, and leave the noun without **il-**:

```ex
هادا كتابي | haada ktaabi | This is my book.
هاي سيارة جديدة | haay sayyaara jdiide | This is a new car.
```

## After the noun

You can also put the full form after a noun with **il-**, especially for "that one":

```ex
الرجّال هداك | ir-rijjaal hadaak | that man (over there)
```

> In Lebanon you'll hear **hayda** (m), **hayde** (f) and **hawdi** (pl) for "this".
