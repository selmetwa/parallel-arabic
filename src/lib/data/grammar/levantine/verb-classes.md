Some verbs follow the conjugation patterns exactly. Others change shape because their root has a **weak letter**: **w**, **y** or a hamza (**ء**). The verb's class tells you which kind it is.

## Sound verbs

Three ordinary consonants, no surprises.

```ex
كتب / كتبت / بيكتب | katab / katabt / byiktob | he wrote / I wrote / he writes
```

## Hollow verbs: w or y in the middle

The middle letter shows up as a long vowel, which **shortens** before endings that start with a consonant.

| | Past (he) | Past (I) | Present (he) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| see | شاف shaaf | شفت shift | بيشوف bishuuf |
| sleep | نام naam | نمت nimt | بينام binaam |
| say | قال 2aal | قلت 2elt | بيقول bi2uul |

## Defective verbs: w or y at the end

The verb ends in a vowel, which merges with the endings:

| | Past (he) | Past (I) | Present (he) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| forget | نسي nisi | نسيت nsiit | بينسى byinsa |
| walk | مشي mishi | مشيت mshiit | بيمشي byimshi |
| speak | حكى 7aka | حكيت 7akeit | بيحكي bi7ki |

## Doubled verbs: the same letter twice

The second and third root letters are the same. Before consonant endings, they add **-ei-**:

```ex
حبّ / حبّيت / بيحبّ | 7abb / 7abbeit / bi7ebb | he loved / I loved / he loves
```

## Irregular verbs

A few very common verbs don't fit any pattern:

```ex
إجا / جيت / بيجي | ija / jiit / byiji | he came / I came / he comes
أكل / بياكل | akal / byaakol | he ate / he eats
أخد / باخد | akhad / baakhod | he took / I take
```

> Each verb in the [verb tables](/levantine/conjugations) is labelled with its class.
