Arabic has a special form for exactly two of something: the dual. In Levantine it's the ending **-ein**.

## Making the dual

```ex
كتاب ← كتابين | ktaab → ktaabein | a book → two books
يوم ← يومين | yoom → yomein | a day → two days
```

Feminine nouns ending in **-a/-e** use **-tein**:

```ex
سيارة ← سيارتين | sayyaara → sayyaartein | a car → two cars
سنة ← سنتين | sine → sintein | a year → two years
مرّة ← مرّتين | marra → marrtein | once → twice
```

## Don't add the number 2

The dual already means "two", so you don't say *tnein ktaab*. To stress "two", add **tnein** after the noun:

```ex
بدي كتابين | baddi ktaabein | I want two books.
بس كتابين تنين | bass ktaabein tnein | just two books
```

## Words that go with a dual

Adjectives and demonstratives after a dual are plural:

```ex
الولدين هدول | il-waladein hadool | these two boys
```

> With many nouns, especially longer or borrowed ones, people also say **tnein** + plural: **tnein talafonaat** (two phones).
