There are two ways to say "X's Y" in Levantine: put the two nouns side by side, or use **taba3** ("belonging to").

## Two nouns side by side (iḍāfa)

Put the thing owned first, then the owner. The first noun **never** takes **il-**.

```ex
بيت أحمد | beit a7mad | Ahmed's house
باب البيت | baab il-beit | the door of the house
مفتاح السيارة | muftaa7 is-sayyaara | the car key
```

If the first noun is feminine, its ending becomes **-it**:

```ex
سيارة أبوي | sayyaarit abuuy | my father's car
```

## taba3: "belonging to"

**taba3** goes after a noun with **il-**. Many speakers use the same form for everything; others make it agree with the thing owned:

| | Form | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| masculine | تبع taba3 | الكتاب تبعي il-ktaab taba3i (my book) |
| feminine | تبعت taba3it | السيارة تبعتي is-sayyaara taba3ti (my car) |
| plural | تبعول taba3ul | الكتب تبعولي il-kutub taba3uli (my books) |

**taba3** takes the possessive endings: **taba3ak** (your), **taba3a** (her), and so on.

## When to use which

- Family and fixed relationships use the side-by-side form: **ukht 3ali** (Ali's sister).
- **taba3** is common with borrowed words and longer phrases: **il-mobaayl taba3i** (my mobile).
- "Whose is this?" is **la-miin haada?** And "it's mine" is **ili** / **taba3i**.

```ex
لمين هالشنطة؟ | la-miin hash-shante? | Whose bag is this?
هي إلي | hiyye ili | It's mine.
```
