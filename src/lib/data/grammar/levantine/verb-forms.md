Most Arabic words grow from a **root** of three consonants that carries a core meaning. A verb **form** is a fixed pattern laid over the root, and each pattern nudges the meaning in a predictable direction.

## Roots

The root **k-t-b** is about writing:

```ex
كتب | katab | he wrote
كتاب | ktaab | book
مكتب | maktab | office, desk
مكتوب | maktuub | written; a letter
```

## The forms Levantine uses most

Grammarians number the patterns with Roman numerals and use **f-3-l** as a placeholder root.

| Form | Pattern | Present | Typical meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | fa3al | yif3ol / yif3al | the basic meaning | katab (write), shirib (drink) |
| II | fa33al | yfa33el | make someone do it; intensive | 3allam (teach), ghayyar (change) |
| III | faa3al | yfaa3el | do it with/to someone | saa3ad (help), 7aawal (try) |
| V | tfa33al | yitfa33al | reflexive of II | t3allam (learn), t7arrak (move) |
| VI | tfaa3al | yitfaa3al | do it to each other | t2aabalu (they met) |
| VII | nfa3al | yinfi3el | get done | nkasar (get broken) |
| VIII | fta3al | yifti3el | self-directed | shtaghal (work), 7taram (respect) |
| X | stfa3al | yistaf3el | seek, consider | sta3mal (use), stanna (wait) |

Form IV (**af3al**) belongs mainly to Modern Standard Arabic. Levantine usually uses Form II or I instead.

## Pairs to spot

```ex
علّم ← تعلّم | 3allam → t3allam | teach → learn (II → V)
فتح ← انفتح | fata7 → nfata7 | open → be opened (I → VII)
```

## Levantine's n- passive

Levantine makes many passives with Form VII: **nfata7** (was opened), **nkatab** (was written), **nsara2** (was stolen). Some speakers use **t-** instead: **tkatab**.
