Levantine uses **iza** for real conditions ("if it rains") and **law** for imagined ones ("if I were rich").

## Real conditions: iza + past

After **iza**, use the **past** tense, even when you're talking about the future. The result usually uses **ra7** or a command.

```ex
إذا إجت، رح قلها | iza ijit, ra7 2illa | If she comes, I'll tell her.
إذا عندك وقت، تعا | iza 3andak wa2t, ta3a | If you have time, come over.
إذا ما إجا، اتصل فيي | iza ma ija, ittasel fiyyi | If he doesn't come, call me.
```

## Imagined conditions: law + kaan

For something unreal, or something that didn't happen, use **law** and put **kaan** (was) in the result:

```ex
لو كنت بعرف، كنت قلتلك | law kint ba3ref, kint 2eltellak | If I had known, I would have told you.
لو معي مصاري، كنت اشتريتها | law ma3i masaari, kint ishtareita | If I had had money, I would have bought it.
```

**kaan** agrees with the person: **kint** (I/you m), **kinti** (you f), **kaan** (he), **kaanit** (she), **kinna** (we), **kintu** (you pl), **kaanu** (they).

## Useful combinations

```ex
حتى لو | 7atta law | even if
إلا إذا | illa iza | unless
إذا بتريد | iza bitriid | please (literally "if you want")
```
