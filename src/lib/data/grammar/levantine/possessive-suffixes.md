Arabic says "my", "your", "his" and so on with short endings attached to the noun. The same endings also mark the object of a verb ("saw *me*") and follow prepositions ("with *me*").

## Possessive endings

| | Ending | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | ـي -i | كتابي ktaabi | my book |
| your (m) | ـك -ak | كتابك ktaabak | your book |
| your (f) | ـك -ik | كتابك ktaabik | your book |
| his | ـه -o | كتابه ktaabo | his book |
| her | ـها -ha | كتابها ktaaba | her book |
| our | ـنا -na | كتابنا ktaabna | our book |
| your (pl) | ـكن -kon | كتابكن ktaabkon | your book |
| their | ـهن -hon | كتابهن ktaabon | their book |

Some things to notice:

- **-ak** and **-ik** are both written **ـك**. Only the vowel tells you who you're talking to.
- **ـه** is said **-o** (in Palestine and Jordan, **-u**).
- The **h** of **-ha** and **-hon** is often dropped after a consonant: **ktaaba**, **ktaabon**.
- After a vowel, "my" is **-yi**: **abuuyi** (my father).
- Feminine **-a/-e** becomes **-it**/**-t**: **sayyaarti** (my car).

## The same endings as objects

After a verb, the endings mean "me, you, him…". "Me" is **-ni**.

```ex
شافني | shaafni | he saw me
شافك | shaafak | he saw you (m)
شافها | shaafa | he saw her
شافهن | shaafon | he saw them
```

## After prepositions

```ex
معي | ma3i | with me
إلك | ilak | for you (m)
عليه | 3alei | on him
منها | minna | from her
```

## "To have" is a preposition

Levantine has no verb "to have". It uses **3and** ("at") plus an ending, or **ma3** ("with") for things you have on you.

```ex
عندي سيارة | 3andi sayyaara | I have a car.
معك مصاري؟ | ma3ak masaari? | Do you (m) have money on you?
ما عنّا وقت | ma 3anna wa2t | We don't have time.
```

> See [possession](/grammar/levantine/possession) for **taba3**, the other common way to say "my".
