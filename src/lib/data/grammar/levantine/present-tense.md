A Levantine present-tense verb has three parts: a **person prefix** that shows who is doing it, the **verb stem**, and usually a **b-** in front for the normal present.

## The bare present and the b- present

Here is **katab** (to write), stem **-ktob**:

| Person | Bare form | With b- |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | iktob | biktob |
| inta (you m) | tiktob | btiktob |
| inti (you f) | tiktbi | btiktbi |
| huwwe (he) | yiktob | byiktob |
| hiyye (she) | tiktob | btiktob |
| ni7na (we) | niktob | mniktob |
| intu (you pl) | tiktbu | btiktbu |
| hinne (they) | yiktbu | byiktbu |

"We" takes **m-** instead of **b-**: **mniktob**. And "you (m)" and "she" share a form.

## b-: the normal present

```ex
بشتغل ببنك | bishtighel b-bank | I work in a bank.
بتحكي عربي؟ | bti7ki 3arabi? | Do you speak Arabic?
```

## 3am: right now

To stress that something is happening at this moment, add **3am** before the verb:

```ex
عم بكتب رسالة | 3am biktob risaale | I'm writing a letter (right now).
شو عم تعمل؟ | shu 3am ta3mel? | What are you doing?
```

## Drop b- after "want", "must", "can"

Use the bare form after these. English uses "to" + verb here; Arabic uses the bare present with the right person.

```ex
بدّي إكتب | baddi iktob | I want to write.
لازم تيجي | laazim tiiji | You have to come.
فيني ساعدك؟ | fiyyi saa3dak? | Can I help you?
```

**badd-** ("want") and **fi-** ("can") take possessive endings: **baddak** (you want), **fiyya** (she can).

> For "will", put **ra7** in front of the bare form. See [the future](/grammar/levantine/future).
