Arabic uses one pattern, **af3al**, for both "bigger" and "the biggest". It doesn't change for gender or number.

## Making the comparative

Take the three root letters of the adjective and put them into **a_ _a_**:

| Adjective | Comparative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كبير kbiir | أكبر akbar | bigger |
| زغير zghiir | أزغر azghar | smaller |
| حلو 7ilu | أحلى a7la | nicer |
| منيح mnii7 | أحسن a7san | better |
| غالي ghaali | أغلى aghla | more expensive |
| رخيص rkhiis | أرخص arkhas | cheaper |
| بعيد b3iid | أبعد ab3ad | further |

**mnii7** (good) takes **a7san**, from a different root.

## "Than" is min

```ex
بيروت أكبر من صيدا | beirut akbar min saida | Beirut is bigger than Sidon.
هيّي أطول منه | hiyye atwal minno | She's taller than him.
هادا أرخص من هداك | haada arkhas min hadaak | This one is cheaper than that one.
```

## The superlative: before the noun

Put the comparative **before** a noun to mean "the most":

```ex
أكبر بيت | akbar beit | the biggest house
أحسن مطعم بالبلد | a7san mat3am bil-balad | the best restaurant in town
```

Or on its own with **il-**: **huwwe il-a7san** (he's the best).

## Adjectives without the pattern

Longer adjectives and participles use **aktar** (more) after the adjective:

```ex
مبسوط أكتر | mabsuut aktar | happier
```
