Arabic uses one pattern, **af3al**, for both "bigger" and "the biggest". In comparisons it doesn't change for gender or number.

## Making the comparative

Take the three root letters of the adjective and put them into **a_ _a_**:

| Adjective | Comparative | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| كبير kabiir | أكبر akbar | bigger |
| صغير saghiir | أصغر asghar | smaller |
| جميل jamiil | أجمل ajmal | more beautiful |
| جيّد jayyid | أفضل afdal / أحسن a7san | better |
| قريب qariib | أقرب aqrab | closer |
| غالٍ ghaalin | أغلى aghlaa | more expensive |

## "Than" is min

```ex
القاهرةُ أكبرُ من الإسكندريةِ | al-qaahiratu akbaru mina al-iskandariyyati | Cairo is bigger than Alexandria.
هي أطولُ منه | hiya atwalu minhu | She is taller than him.
```

## The superlative

Put the comparative **before** an indefinite singular noun (an iḍāfa):

```ex
أكبرُ مدينةٍ | akbaru madiinatin | the biggest city
أفضلُ طالبٍ في الصفِّ | afdalu taalibin fii as-saffi | the best student in the class
```

Or use it with **al-** after a noun. In this use it does agree, with a special feminine form **fu3laa**:

```ex
الطالبُ الأكبرُ | at-taalibu al-akbaru | the oldest student
البنتُ الكبرى | al-bintu al-kubraa | the eldest girl
```

## Adjectives without the pattern

Longer adjectives, colours and participles use **akthar** (more) or **ashadd** (more intensely) + an accusative noun:

```ex
أكثرُ أهميةً | aktharu ahammiyyatan | more important
أشدُّ حُمرةً | ashaddu 7umratan | redder
```
