The Fusha present tense (the "imperfect") is built from a **person prefix**, the **verb stem**, and an ending. It covers both "I write" and "I am writing".

## The present

Here is **kataba** (to write), stem **-ktub-**:

| Person | Arabic | Transliteration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ana (I) | أكتبُ | aktubu |
| anta (you m) | تكتبُ | taktubu |
| anti (you f) | تكتبين | taktubiina |
| huwa (he) | يكتبُ | yaktubu |
| hiya (she) | تكتبُ | taktubu |
| antumaa (you two) | تكتبان | taktubaani |
| humaa (they two, m) | يكتبان | yaktubaani |
| na7nu (we) | نكتبُ | naktubu |
| antum (you pl m) | تكتبون | taktubuuna |
| antunna (you pl f) | تكتبْنَ | taktubna |
| hum (they m) | يكتبون | yaktubuuna |
| hunna (they f) | يكتبْنَ | yaktubna |

The vowel in the middle of the stem (**u** in **yaktubu**, **a** in **yashrabu**, **i** in **yajlisu**) has to be learned with each Form I verb. Dictionaries give it.

```ex
أعملُ في بنكٍ | a3malu fii bankin | I work in a bank.
ماذا تقرأين؟ | maadha taqra2iina? | What are you (f) reading?
```

## Three moods

The ending changes after certain words:

| Mood | Ending | Used after | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| indicative | -u | normally | yaktubu (he writes) |
| subjunctive | -a | an (to), lan (won't), li- (in order to) | an yaktuba (to write) |
| jussive | (none) | lam (didn't), laa (don't) | lam yaktub (he didn't write) |

In the subjunctive and jussive, the **-na/-ni** endings drop: **taktubiina** becomes **an taktubii**.

## "Want to", "must", "can"

Use **an** + the subjunctive:

```ex
أريدُ أن أكتبَ | uriidu an aktuba | I want to write.
يجبُ أن تأتيَ | yajibu an ta2tiya | You must come.
هل يمكنُ أن نتكلّمَ؟ | hal yumkinu an natakallama? | Can we talk?
```

> For "will", put **sa-** in front of the present. See [the future](/grammar/fusha/future).
