Arabic says "my", "your", "his" and so on with endings attached to the noun. The same endings also mark the object of a verb ("saw *me*") and follow prepositions ("with *me*").

## Possessive endings

| | Ending | Example | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | ـي -ii | كتابي kitaabii | my book |
| your (m) | ـكَ -ka | كتابُكَ kitaabuka | your book |
| your (f) | ـكِ -ki | كتابُكِ kitaabuki | your book |
| his | ـهُ -hu | كتابُهُ kitaabuhu | his book |
| her | ـها -haa | كتابُها kitaabuhaa | her book |
| your (two) | ـكما -kumaa | كتابُكما kitaabukumaa | your book |
| their (two) | ـهما -humaa | كتابُهما kitaabuhumaa | their book |
| our | ـنا -naa | كتابُنا kitaabunaa | our book |
| your (pl m) | ـكم -kum | كتابُكم kitaabukum | your book |
| your (pl f) | ـكنّ -kunna | كتابُكنّ kitaabukunna | your book |
| their (m) | ـهم -hum | كتابُهم kitaabuhum | their book |
| their (f) | ـهنّ -hunna | كتابُهنّ kitaabuhunna | their book |

The vowel before the ending (**-u-** above) is the noun's case ending.

- **-hu** becomes **-hi** after **i** or **y**: **fii baytihi** (in his house).
- Feminine **ة** becomes **t**: **sayyaaratii** (my car).

## The same endings as objects

After a verb, the endings mean "me, you, him…". "Me" is **-nii**.

```ex
رآني | ra2aanii | he saw me
رآكَ | ra2aaka | he saw you (m)
رآها | ra2aahaa | he saw her
رآهم | ra2aahum | he saw them
```

## After prepositions

```ex
معي | ma3ii | with me
لكَ | laka | for you (m)
عليه | 3alayhi | on him
منها | minhaa | from her
```

## "To have"

Fusha has no verb "to have". It uses **3inda** ("at"), **ladaa** ("at, with") or **li-** ("for") plus an ending:

```ex
عندي سيارةٌ | 3indii sayyaaratun | I have a car.
لديك وقتٌ؟ | ladayka waqtun? | Do you (m) have time?
لي أخٌ | lii akhun | I have a brother.
```
