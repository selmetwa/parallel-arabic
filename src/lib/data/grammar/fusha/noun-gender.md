Every noun in Arabic is either masculine or feminine. The gender matters because adjectives, verbs, pronouns and words like "this" have to match it.

## Spotting a feminine noun

Most feminine nouns end in **ة** (taa marbuuta), pronounced **-a** at the end of a phrase and **-at** inside one.

```ex
مدرسة | madrasa | school (f)
سيارة | sayyaara | car (f)
مدينة | madiina | city (f)
```

Two other feminine endings are **ى** (-aa) and **اء** (-aa2): **dhikraa** (memory), **sa7raa2** (desert).

## Feminine nouns without an ending

- female people: **umm** (mother), **bint** (girl)
- paired body parts: **3ayn** (eye), **yad** (hand), **rijl** (leg), **udhun** (ear)
- names of most countries and cities: **misr** (Egypt), **dimashq** (Damascus)
- a few others: **shams** (sun), **ard** (earth), **7arb** (war), **daar** (house)

## Making a feminine form

People and adjectives add **ة**:

```ex
مُعلّم / مُعلّمة | mu3allim / mu3allima | teacher (m / f)
مصريّ / مصريّة | misriyy / misriyya | Egyptian (m / f)
كبير / كبيرة | kabiir / kabiira | big (m / f)
```

## The ة is pronounced -at before an owner

```ex
سيارةُ أحمدَ | sayyaaratu a7mada | Ahmed's car
سيارتي | sayyaaratii | my car
```

## Why it matters

```ex
الولدُ طويلٌ | al-waladu tawiilun | The boy is tall.
البنتُ طويلةٌ | al-bintu tawiilatun | The girl is tall.
```

> Plurals of *things* (not people) are grammatically feminine singular: **al-kutubu jadiidatun** (the books are new).
