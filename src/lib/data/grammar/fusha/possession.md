Fusha shows possession by putting two nouns side by side. This construction is called **iḍāfa** ("addition"), and it does the work of English "'s" and "of".

## The iḍāfa

Put the thing owned first, then the owner. The rules:

- The **first** noun never takes **al-** and never takes **-n** (nunation), even though it means "the".
- The **second** noun is in the genitive case (ending **-i**).

```ex
بيتُ أحمدَ | baytu a7mada | Ahmed's house
بابُ البيتِ | baabu al-bayti | the door of the house
مفتاحُ السيارةِ | miftaa7u as-sayyaarati | the car key
```

If the first noun ends in **ة**, the **t** is pronounced:

```ex
مدينةُ القاهرةِ | madiinatu al-qaahirati | the city of Cairo
```

## Chains

You can link more than two nouns. Only the last one can take **al-**:

```ex
بابُ بيتِ المُعلّمِ | baabu bayti al-mu3allimi | the door of the teacher's house
```

## Adjectives come after the whole iḍāfa

An adjective describing either noun goes at the end and agrees with the noun it describes:

```ex
بيتُ أحمدَ الكبيرُ | baytu a7mada al-kabiiru | Ahmed's big house
```

## "Belonging to": li-

To say "a book of Ahmed's" (indefinite), use **li-**:

```ex
كتابٌ لأحمدَ | kitaabun li-a7mada | a book of Ahmed's
هذا الكتابُ لي | haadha al-kitaabu lii | This book is mine.
```
