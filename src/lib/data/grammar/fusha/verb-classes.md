Some verbs follow the conjugation patterns exactly. Others change shape because their root has a **weak letter**: **w**, **y** or a hamza (**ء**). The verb's class tells you which kind it is.

## Sound verbs

Three ordinary consonants, no surprises.

```ex
كتب / كتبتُ / يكتبُ | kataba / katabtu / yaktubu | he wrote / I wrote / he writes
```

## Assimilated verbs: w at the start

The **w** drops out of the present of most Form I verbs:

```ex
وصل / يصلُ | wasala / yasilu | he arrived / he arrives
وجد / يجدُ | wajada / yajidu | he found / he finds
```

## Hollow verbs: w or y in the middle

The middle letter shows up as a long **aa** in the past, and **shortens** before endings that start with a consonant:

| | Past (he) | Past (I) | Present (he) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| say | قال qaala | قلتُ qultu | يقولُ yaquulu |
| sell | باع baa3a | بعتُ bi3tu | يبيعُ yabii3u |
| sleep | نام naama | نمتُ nimtu | ينامُ yanaamu |

## Defective verbs: w or y at the end

The verb ends in a vowel, which merges with the endings:

| | Past (he) | Past (I) | Present (he) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| walk | مشى mashaa | مشيتُ mashaytu | يمشي yamshii |
| call | دعا da3aa | دعوتُ da3awtu | يدعو yad3uu |
| forget | نسي nasiya | نسيتُ nasiitu | ينسى yansaa |

## Doubled verbs: the same letter twice

When an ending starts with a consonant, the doubled letter splits:

```ex
ظنّ / ظننتُ / يظنُّ | zanna / zanantu / yazunnu | he thought / I thought / he thinks
```

## Hamzated verbs

A hamza in the root is spelled on different "seats" (أ، ؤ، ئ، ء) depending on the vowels around it, but conjugates mostly like a sound verb:

```ex
سأل / سألتُ / يسألُ | sa2ala / sa2altu / yas2alu | he asked / I asked / he asks
أكل / يأكلُ / كُلْ | akala / ya2kulu / kul | he ate / he eats / eat!
```
