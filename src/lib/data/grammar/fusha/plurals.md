Arabic makes plurals in two ways: by adding an ending (a "sound" plural), or by changing the vowels inside the word (a "broken" plural).

## Sound masculine plural: -uun / -iin

For male people and occupations. The ending changes with the noun's case: **-uuna** as the subject, **-iina** otherwise.

```ex
مُعلّمون | mu3allimuuna | teachers (subject)
رأيتُ المُعلّمين | ra2aytu al-mu3allimiina | I saw the teachers.
```

## Sound feminine plural: -aat

For most feminine nouns and many borrowed or technical words:

```ex
سيارة ← سيارات | sayyaara → sayyaaraat | car → cars
جامعة ← جامعات | jaami3a → jaami3aat | university → universities
```

## Broken plurals

Most everyday nouns have a broken plural. The root letters stay, but the pattern of vowels changes.

| Singular | Plural | Pattern | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| كتاب kitaab | كُتُب kutub | fu3ul | book |
| بيت bayt | بيوت buyuut | fu3uul | house |
| ولد walad | أولاد awlaad | af3aal | boy, child |
| رجل rajul | رجال rijaal | fi3aal | man |
| مدينة madiina | مُدُن mudun | fu3ul | city |
| طالب taalib | طلاب tullaab | fu33aal | student |
| مسجد masjid | مساجد masaajid | mafaa3il | mosque |

There's no reliable way to predict which pattern a noun takes, so learn each plural with its singular. Dictionaries list both.

## Collective nouns

Some nouns refer to a kind of thing in general. Add **ة** for one item:

```ex
شجر / شجرة / أشجار | shajar / shajara / ashjaar | trees / a tree / trees (counted)
```

> Numbers change which form you use: 3 to 10 take the plural, 11 and up take the singular. See [counting things](/grammar/fusha/numbers-counting).
