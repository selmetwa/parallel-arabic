# Saudi (Hijazi) phrasebook. ث/ذ written ت/د as pronounced in everyday words; ق said "g" in the transliteration.

## hello
هَلا | hala | welcome
usage: The everyday Hijazi hello, warm and fine for anyone. In Jeddah a greeting is usually followed by "kaifak?" and a few blessings before getting to the point.
V: formal / traditional | السَّلامُ عَلَيكُم | is-salaamu 3alaikum | peace be upon you | The standard greeting when entering a shop, office or home; always answered with "w-3alaikum is-salaam".
V: extra warm | يا هَلا | ya hala | oh welcome | Warm and very common when you see a friend.
V: casual | أهلين | ahlain | two welcomes | Friendly and relaxed, typical of Hijaz.
R: هَلا بيك | hala beek | welcome to you
R: أهلين وسَهلين | ahlain w-sahlain | welcome, welcome
E: هَلا، كيفَك اليوم؟ | hala, kaifak il-yoom? | Hello, how are you today?
E: يا هَلا، إيش الأخبار؟ | ya hala, eesh il-akhbaar? | Hello, what's new?
E: هَلا، أحمد موجود؟ | hala, a7mad mawjood? | Hello, is Ahmed here?

## how-are-you
كيفَك؟ | kaifak? | how are you?
usage: The standard Hijazi "how are you?" to one man; to a woman "kaifik?", to a group "kaifukum?". "Eesh lawnak?" (what's your colour?) is a warm local alternative.
V: to a woman | كيفِك؟ | kaifik? | how are you? (f) | The feminine form.
V: Hijazi | إيش لونَك؟ | eesh loonak? | what is your colour? | Warm, traditional Hijazi way to ask.
V: news | إيش أخبارَك؟ | eesh akhbaarak? | what's your news? | Casual "what's up?".
R: بخير، الحَمدُلله | b-khair, il-7amdulillah | well, praise God
R: تَمام، وإنتَ كيفَك؟ | tamaam, w-inta kaifak? | fine, and how are you?
E: هَلا خالِد، كيفَك؟ | hala khaalid, kaifak? | Hi Khalid, how are you?
E: كيفِك يا خالة؟ | kaifik ya khaala? | How are you, auntie?
E: كيف الأهل؟ | kaif il-ahl? | How's the family?

## thank-you
شُكراً | shukran | thanks
usage: The everyday thank you. Hijazis very often add "ya3teek il-3aafya" (God give you health), especially to someone who has worked or helped.
V: blessing | يِعطيك العافية | ya3teek il-3aafya | may He give you health | Very common thanks for any effort.
V: grateful | مَشكور | mashkoor | thanked | Polite, to a man; to a woman "mashkoora".
V: to someone who cooked | تِسلَم يَدَك | tislam yadak | bless your hand | After a meal or a favour.
R: العَفو | il-3afw | you're welcome
R: الله يعافيك | allah y3aafeek | God give you health too
E: شُكراً على الأكل | shukran 3ala il-akl | Thanks for the food
E: يِعطيك العافية، ما قَصَّرت | ya3teek il-3aafya, ma gassart | God bless you, you did plenty
E: مَشكور على المُساعدة | mashkoor 3ala il-musaa3ada | Thanks for the help

## happy-birthday
عيد ميلاد سَعيد | 3eed meelaad sa3eed | happy feast of birth
usage: The standard birthday wish. Hijazis also say "kull sana w-inta tayyib", the same wish used for Eid and other yearly occasions.
V: yearly blessing | كُل سَنة وإنتَ طَيِّب | kull sana w-inta tayyib | every year and you are well | For birthdays and holidays; to a woman "w-inti tayyiba".
V: long life | عُقبال المِيّة | 3ugbaal il-miyya | may you reach a hundred | A playful long-life wish.
R: وإنتَ طَيِّب | w-inta tayyib | and you are well
R: شُكراً، الله يخَلّيك | shukran, allah ykhalleek | thanks, God keep you
E: عيد ميلاد سَعيد يا حَبيبي! | 3eed meelaad sa3eed ya 7abeebi! | Happy birthday, my dear!
E: كُل سَنة وإنتِ طَيّبة، عُقبال المِيّة | kull sana w-inti tayyiba, 3ugbaal il-miyya | Many happy returns, may you reach a hundred
E: جِبت لَك هَدية | jibt lak hadiyya | I brought you a gift

## good-morning
صَباح الخير | sabaa7 il-khair | morning of goodness
usage: The normal morning greeting. The reply adds a nicer morning: light, roses or jasmine.
V: reply | صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light | The classic reply.
V: Hijazi flourish | صَباح الفُل | sabaa7 il-full | morning of jasmine | Jasmine (full) is a symbol of Hijaz.
R: صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light
R: صَباح الوَرد | sabaa7 il-ward | morning of roses
E: صَباح الخير، كيفَك اليوم؟ | sabaa7 il-khair, kaifak il-yoom? | Good morning, how are you today?
E: صَباح الخير، الفُطور جاهِز | sabaa7 il-khair, il-futoor jaahiz | Good morning, breakfast is ready
E: صَباح الخير يا جيران | sabaa7 il-khair ya jeeraan | Good morning, neighbours

## good-evening
مَسا الخير | masa il-khair | evening of goodness
usage: The greeting from late afternoon on. The reply adds a nicer word.
V: reply | مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light | The standard reply.
V: Hijazi | مَسا الفُل | masa il-full | evening of jasmine | Warm and local.
R: مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light
R: مَسا الوَرد | masa il-ward | evening of roses
E: مَسا الخير، إيش الأخبار؟ | masa il-khair, eesh il-akhbaar? | Good evening, what's new?
E: مَسا الخير، اِتفَضَّلوا | masa il-khair, itfadhdhalu | Good evening, please come in
E: مَسا الخير يا شَباب | masa il-khair ya shabaab | Good evening, guys

## goodbye
مَعَ السَّلامة | ma3a is-salaama | with safety
usage: The standard goodbye. Hijazis often add "fi amaan allah" or "sallim 3ala il-ahl".
V: blessing | في أمان الله | fi amaan allah | in God's safekeeping | Warm, especially to travellers.
V: casual | يَلّا باي | yalla baay | OK, bye | Common among young people.
R: الله يِسَلمَك | allah ysallimak | God keep you safe
R: في أمان الله | fi amaan allah | in God's safekeeping
E: يَلّا، مَعَ السَّلامة | yalla, ma3a is-salaama | OK, goodbye
E: مَعَ السَّلامة، سَلِّم على الأهل | ma3a is-salaama, sallim 3ala il-ahl | Goodbye, greet the family
E: توصَل بالسَّلامة | toosal bis-salaama | Arrive safely

## please
لو سَمَحت | law sama7t | if you allow
usage: The everyday please when asking for something. "Min fadhlak" is a bit more formal; "allah ykhalleek" is warm.
V: formal | مِن فَضلَك | min fadhlak | from your kindness | Polite, for shops and offices.
V: warm | الله يخَلّيك | allah ykhalleek | may God keep you | Friendly and heartfelt.
V: to a woman | لو سَمَحتي | law sama7ti | please (f) | The feminine form.
R: حاضِر | 7aadhir | right away
R: اِتفَضَّل | itfadhdhal | here you go
E: لو سَمَحت، إدّيني مُويَة | law sama7t, iddeeni mooya | Water please
E: مِن فَضلَك، فين المَحَطّة؟ | min fadhlak, feen il-ma7atta? | Excuse me please, where is the station?
E: الله يخَلّيك، ساعِدني | allah ykhalleek, saa3idni | Please, help me

## excuse-me
لو سَمَحت | law sama7t | if you allow
usage: Use it to get attention or ask a stranger. To a woman "law sama7ti". "3an iznak" is used when passing.
V: pardon | عَفواً | 3afwan | pardon | Before interrupting.
V: let me pass | عَن إدنَك | 3an idnak | with your permission | When passing between people.
R: اِتفَضَّل | itfadhdhal | go ahead
R: نَعَم؟ | na3am? | yes?
E: لو سَمَحت، فين الحَمّام؟ | law sama7t, feen il-7ammaam? | Excuse me, where is the bathroom?
E: عَن إدنَك، أبغى أعَدّي | 3an idnak, abgha a3addi | Excuse me, I want to pass
E: عَفواً، دي السَّيّارة حَقّتَك؟ | 3afwan, di is-sayyaara 7aggatak? | Excuse me, is this car yours?

## im-sorry
آسِف | aasif | sorry
usage: The general apology from a man; a woman says "aasfa". "Ma 3alaish" or "sma7ni" are everyday light apologies.
V: female speaker | آسفة | aasfa | sorry (f) | Said by a woman.
V: forgive me | سامِحني | saami7ni | forgive me | For something more serious.
V: light | مَعليش | ma3laish | never mind | Light apology for small things.
R: ولا يهِمَّك | wala yhimmak | don't worry
R: مافي مُشكِلة | maafi mushkila | no problem
E: آسِف، اِتأخَّرت | aasif, it2akhkhart | Sorry, I'm late
E: آسفة، ما سِمِعتَك | aasfa, ma simi3tak | Sorry, I didn't hear you
E: سامِحني، ما كان قَصدي | saami7ni, ma kaan gasdi | Forgive me, I didn't mean it

## yes-and-no
أيوا ولا | aiwa w-la | yes and no
usage: "Aiwa" is the everyday Hijazi yes and "la" the no. "Na3am" is a polite yes, and "abshir" means "sure, consider it done".
V: polite yes | نَعَم | na3am | yes | Respectful, also to answer when called.
V: of course | أبشِر | abshir | rejoice | Sure, I'll do it.
V: firm no | لا والله | la wallah | no by God | Emphatic no, or polite refusal of an offer.
R: تَمام | tamaam | fine
R: طَيِّب | tayyib | OK
E: أيوا، أبغى شاهي | aiwa, abgha shaahi | Yes, I want tea
E: لا، شُكراً | la, shukran | No, thank you
E: أبشِر، حَجي مَعاك | abshir, 7aji ma3aak | Sure, I'll come with you

## my-name-is
إسمي | ismi | my name
usage: Say "ismi" then your name. The reply is often "tsharrafna" (we're honoured).
V: introducing yourself | أنا إسمي | ana ismi | I, my name is | Fuller form.
V: honoured | تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | we are honoured | The usual answer.
R: تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | nice to meet you
R: أهلين، أنا إسمي فَيصَل | ahlain, ana ismi faisal | hi, my name is Faisal
E: هَلا، إسمي سارة | hala, ismi saara | Hi, my name is Sarah
E: أنا إسمي مايكِل، مِن لَندَن | ana ismi maaykil, min landan | My name is Michael, from London
E: إسمي رَيم، تشَرَّفنا | ismi reem, tsharrafna | My name is Reem, nice to meet you

## whats-your-name
إيش إسمَك؟ | eesh ismak? | what is your name?
usage: The usual question to a man; to a woman "eesh ismik?".
V: to a woman | إيش إسمِك؟ | eesh ismik? | what's your name? (f) | The feminine form.
V: polite | مُمكِن أعرِف إسمَك؟ | mumkin a3rif ismak? | may I know your name? | More careful, for formal situations.
R: إسمي فَيصَل | ismi faisal | my name is Faisal
R: أنا نورة | ana noora | I'm Noura
E: هَلا، إيش إسمَك؟ | hala, eesh ismak? | Hi, what's your name?
E: إيش إسمِك يا حِلوة؟ | eesh ismik ya 7ilwa? | What's your name, sweetie?
E: إيش إسم وَلَدَك؟ | eesh ism waladak? | What's your son's name?

## where-are-you-from
مِن فين إنتَ؟ | min feen inta? | from where are you?
usage: To a man "min feen inta?", to a woman "min feen inti?". Jeddah is full of people from everywhere, so this is a very common question.
V: to a woman | مِن فين إنتِ؟ | min feen inti? | where are you from? (f) | The feminine form.
V: which city | مِن أيّ مَدينة؟ | min ayy madeena? | from which city? | Asked after the country.
R: أنا مِن جِدّة | ana min jidda | I'm from Jeddah
R: مِن مَكّة | min makka | from Mecca
E: مِن فين إنتَ؟ إنتَ مو سُعودي | min feen inta? inta moo su3oodi | Where are you from? You're not Saudi
E: أنا مِن أمريكا، وإنتَ؟ | ana min amreeka, w-inta? | I'm from America, and you?
E: أهلي مِن المَدينة | ahli min il-madeena | My family is from Medina

## i-dont-understand
ما فِهِمت | ma fihimt | I didn't understand
usage: The natural Hijazi way to say you don't understand. Add "3eed" (repeat) or "b-shwaish" (slowly).
V: repeat please | عيد لو سَمَحت | 3eed law sama7t | repeat please | Ask someone to say it again.
V: slower | بشْوَيش | b-shwaish | slowly | Ask someone to speak slowly.
R: طَيِّب، أعيدها | tayyib, a3eedha | OK, I'll repeat it
R: مافي مُشكِلة | maafi mushkila | no problem
E: آسِف، ما فِهِمت | aasif, ma fihimt | Sorry, I didn't understand
E: ما فِهِمت، اِتكَلَّم بشْوَيش | ma fihimt, itkallam b-shwaish | I didn't understand, speak slowly
E: ما فِهِمت إيش قُلت | ma fihimt eesh gult | I didn't understand what you said

## do-you-speak-english
تِتكَلَّم إنجليزي؟ | titkallam ingleezi? | do you speak English?
usage: To a man "titkallam", to a woman "titkallami". Many Saudis, especially in Jeddah, speak English.
V: to a woman | تِتكَلَّمي إنجليزي؟ | titkallami ingleezi? | do you speak English? (f) | The feminine form.
V: a little | شْوَيّة | shwayya | a little | A common answer.
R: أيوا، شْوَيّة | aiwa, shwayya | yes, a little
R: أكيد | akeed | of course
E: لو سَمَحت، تِتكَلَّم إنجليزي؟ | law sama7t, titkallam ingleezi? | Excuse me, do you speak English?
E: أنا أتكَلَّم عَرَبي شْوَيّة | ana atkallam 3arabi shwayya | I speak a little Arabic
E: فيه أحَد يِتكَلَّم إنجليزي هِنا؟ | feeh a7ad yitkallam ingleezi hina? | Is there anyone who speaks English here?

## how-much-is-this
بكَم دا؟ | bikam da? | for how much is this?
usage: The everyday shopping question in Jeddah. "Da" is the Hijazi "this" (m), "di" for feminine nouns. Bargaining is normal in Al-Balad's markets.
V: feminine item | بكَم دي؟ | bikam di? | how much is this? (f) | For feminine nouns like an abaya.
V: discount | فيه خَصم؟ | feeh khasm? | is there a discount? | Opening line for bargaining.
R: بعَشَرة ريال | b-3ashara riyaal | ten riyals
R: لَك بسَبعة | lak b-sab3a | for you, seven
E: بكَم دا القَميص؟ | bikam da il-gamees? | How much is this shirt?
E: غالي! فيه خَصم؟ | ghaali! feeh khasm? | Expensive! Is there a discount?
E: بكَم كيلو الطَّماطِم؟ | bikam keelo it-tamaatim? | How much is a kilo of tomatoes?

## i-love-you
أحِبَّك | a7ibbak | I love you
usage: To a man "a7ibbak", to a woman "a7ibbik". Hijazi endearments include "ya galbi" (my heart) and "ya 3umri" (my life).
V: to a woman | أحِبِّك | a7ibbik | I love you (f) | The feminine form.
V: affection | يا قَلبي | ya galbi | my heart | Very common endearment.
V: deep | أموت فيك | amoot feek | I die in you | Strong, romantic.
R: وأنا أحِبَّك | w-ana a7ibbak | and I love you
R: وأنا أكتَر | w-ana aktar | and I more
E: أحِبَّك مَرّة | a7ibbak marra | I love you so much
E: ماما، أحِبِّك | maama, a7ibbik | Mum, I love you
E: إنتِ عُمري | inti 3umri | You're my life

## congratulations
مَبروك | mabrook | blessed
usage: For any happy news. The reply is "allah ybaarik feek" (God bless you).
V: more | ألف مَبروك | alf mabrook | a thousand congratulations | Extra warm.
V: new baby | الحَمدُلله على السَّلامة | il-7amdulillah 3ala is-salaama | thank God for safety | Said to a new mother.
R: الله يبارِك فيك | allah ybaarik feek | God bless you
R: عُقبالَك | 3ugbaalak | may it be your turn
E: مَبروك الوَظيفة الجَديدة! | mabrook il-wadheefa ij-jadeeda! | Congratulations on the new job!
E: ألف مَبروك على البيت | alf mabrook 3ala il-bait | Congratulations on the house
E: مَبروك، عُقبال الزَّواج | mabrook, 3ugbaal iz-zawaaj | Congratulations, may the wedding come next

## welcome
حَيّاك الله | 7ayyaak allah | may God greet you
usage: The classic Saudi welcome to a guest, to one man; to a woman "7ayyaaki allah", to a group "7ayyaakum allah". Coffee and dates follow.
V: to a group | حَيّاكُم الله | 7ayyaakum allah | welcome all | For several guests.
V: standard | أهلاً وسَهلاً | ahlan w-sahlan | family and ease | Understood everywhere.
R: الله يحَيّيك | allah y7ayyeek | God greet you
R: الله يِسَلمَك | allah ysallimak | God keep you safe
E: حَيّاك الله، اِتفَضَّل | 7ayyaak allah, itfadhdhal | Welcome, come in
E: حَيّاكُم الله في جِدّة | 7ayyaakum allah fi jidda | Welcome to Jeddah
E: البيت بيتَك | il-bait baitak | My house is your house

## coffee
قَهوة | gahwa | coffee
usage: Saudi coffee (gahwa) is light, with cardamom and sometimes saffron, served in small cups with dates. Jeddah also has a huge specialty-coffee scene. Shake the cup gently when you've had enough.
V: Saudi coffee | قَهوة سُعودية | gahwa su3oodiyya | Saudi coffee | Served to guests with dates.
V: enough | بَس، شُكراً | bas, shukran | enough, thanks | Said while shaking the cup.
R: تِبغى قَهوة؟ | tibgha gahwa? | do you want coffee?
R: بسُكَّر وَلّا بِدون؟ | b-sukkar walla bidoon? | with sugar or without?
E: أبغى قَهوة لو سَمَحت | abgha gahwa law sama7t | I'd like a coffee please
E: القَهوة السُّعودية مَعَ تَمر | il-gahwa is-su3oodiyya ma3a tamr | Saudi coffee with dates
E: تِشرَب قَهوة وَلّا شاهي؟ | tishrab gahwa walla shaahi? | Do you drink coffee or tea?

## family
عيلة | 3eela | family
usage: "3eela" is the family; "ahl" are your folks. Family gatherings at the grandparents' house, often on Fridays, are central to Hijazi life.
V: folks | أهل | ahl | folks | Your parents and wider family.
V: kids | عِيال | 3iyaal | kids | Your children.
R: الحَمدُلله، كُلُّهُم بخير | il-7amdulillah, kulluhum b-khair | thank God, all are well
R: يِسَلّموا عَلَيك | ysallimu 3alaik | they send their regards
E: عِندي عيلة كبيرة | 3indi 3eela kbeera | I have a big family
E: كيف العيلة؟ | kaif il-3eela? | How's the family?
E: كُل جُمعة نِتجَمَّع في بيت جِدّي | kull jum3a nitjamma3 fi bait jiddi | Every Friday we gather at grandpa's house

## whats-the-weather-like
كيف الجَو؟ | kaif ij-jaw? | how is the weather?
usage: Weather talk in Jeddah is mostly about heat and humidity; winter is the pleasant season.
V: today | كيف الجَو اليوم؟ | kaif ij-jaw il-yoom? | how's the weather today? | Asking about today.
V: humid | رُطوبة | rutooba | humidity | A constant Jeddah topic.
R: حَر مَرّة | 7arr marra | very hot
R: الجَو حِلو | ij-jaw 7ilu | the weather is nice
E: كيف الجَو في جِدّة؟ | kaif ij-jaw fi jidda? | What's the weather like in Jeddah?
E: الجَو حِلو في الشِّتا | ij-jaw 7ilu fish-shita | The weather is nice in winter
E: اليوم فيه غُبار | il-yoom feeh ghubaar | Today there's dust

## im-busy
أنا مَشغول | ana mashghool | I am busy
usage: A man says "mashghool", a woman "mashghoola". It is softened with an apology or a promise to call back.
V: female speaker | أنا مَشغولة | ana mashghoola | I'm busy (f) | Said by a woman.
V: right now | دحين ما أقدَر | da7een ma agdar | now I can't | "Da7een" is the Hijazi "now".
R: مافي مُشكِلة | maafi mushkila | no problem
R: طَيِّب، أكَلِّمَك بَعدين | tayyib, akallimak ba3dain | OK, I'll call you later
E: آسِف، أنا مَشغول دحين | aasif, ana mashghool da7een | Sorry, I'm busy now
E: مَشغولة بالدَّوام | mashghoola bid-dawaam | I'm busy with work
E: إذا مو مَشغول، تَعال | iza moo mashghool, ta3aal | If you're not busy, come

## see-you-later
أشوفَك بَعدين | ashoofak ba3dain | I see you later
usage: To a man "ashoofak", to a woman "ashoofik". Often with "in shaa2 allah".
V: tomorrow | أشوفَك بُكرة | ashoofak bukra | see you tomorrow | "Bukra" is the Hijazi "tomorrow".
V: to a woman | أشوفِك بَعدين | ashoofik ba3dain | see you later (f) | The feminine form.
R: إن شاء الله | in shaa2 allah | God willing
