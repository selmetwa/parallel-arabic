# Iraqi (Baghdadi) phrasebook. Standard letters only; ك said "ch" and ق said "g" in the transliteration.

## hello
هَلا | hala | welcome
usage: The everyday Iraqi hello, warm and fine for anyone, man or woman, friend or stranger. Iraqis rarely stop at one word: a hello is usually followed straight away by "shlonak?" and a round of blessings.
V: formal / traditional | السَّلامُ عَلَيكُم | is-salaamu 3alaikum | peace be upon you | The standard greeting when entering a shop, an office or a room full of people; always answered with "w-3alaikum is-salaam".
V: extra warm | هَلا بيك | hala beek | welcome to you | Adds warmth when greeting one man; to a woman say "hala beech", to a group "hala beekum".
V: casual | هَلو | hallo | hello | Borrowed from English and very common among young people in Baghdad.
R: هَلا بيك | hala beek | welcome to you
R: هَلا وغَلا | hala w-ghala | welcome, dear one
E: هَلا، شلونَك اليوم؟ | hala, shloonak il-yoom? | Hello, how are you today?
E: هَلا عيوني، شنو الأخبار؟ | hala 3yooni, shinu il-akhbaar? | Hello my dear, what's new?
E: هَلا، أحمد موجود؟ | hala, a7mad mawjood? | Hello, is Ahmed here?

## how-are-you
شلونَك؟ | shloonak? | what is your colour?
usage: The standard Iraqi "how are you?" to one man. Its literal meaning is "what's your colour (complexion)?". To a woman say "shloonich?", to a group "shloonkum?". Iraqis often ask it several times in one greeting.
V: to a woman | شلونِك؟ | shloonich? | how are you? (f) | The feminine form, used with any woman.
V: to a group | شلونكُم؟ | shloonkum? | how are you all? | For two or more people, or politely to a family.
V: what's new | شكو ماكو؟ | shaku maku? | what's there, what's not? | The famous Iraqi "what's up?", casual and friendly.
R: زين، الحَمدِلله | zein, il-7amdilla | good, praise God
R: تَمام، وإنتَ شلونَك؟ | tamaam, w-inta shloonak? | fine, and how are you?
E: هَلا خالِد، شلونَك؟ | hala khaalid, shloonak? | Hi Khalid, how are you?
E: شلونِك يا خالة؟ | shloonich ya khaala? | How are you, auntie?
E: شلون الأهل؟ | shloon il-ahal? | How's the family?

## thank-you
شُكراً | shukran | thanks
usage: The everyday thank you, polite and neutral. Iraqis often add a blessing or answer thanks with a blessing, so you will hear "tislam" and "allah ysallmak" as much as "shukran".
V: warm | تِسلَم | tislam | may you be safe | Very common Iraqi thanks to a man; to a woman "tislamiin".
V: to someone who worked for you | عاشَت إيدَك | 3aashat eedak | long live your hand | Said after a meal, a favour or a job well done.
V: strong | مَمنون | mamnoon | grateful | A slightly more formal "I'm grateful".
R: العَفو | il-3afu | you're welcome
R: ولو، ما سَوّينا شي | walaw, ma sawweina shi | not at all, we did nothing
E: شُكراً على الأكل | shukran 3ala il-akil | Thanks for the food
E: عاشَت إيدَك، الأكل يِجَنِّن | 3aashat eedak, il-akil yjannin | Bless your hand, the food is amazing
E: تِسلَم، ما قَصَّرت | tislam, ma gassart | Thank you, you did more than enough

## happy-birthday
عيد ميلاد سَعيد | 3eed meelaad sa3eed | happy feast of birth
usage: The standard birthday wish, understood everywhere. Iraqis add the general blessing "kull sana w-inta saalim" and wish the person a long life.
V: blessing | كُل سَنة وإنتَ سالِم | kull sana w-inta saalim | every year and you are well | Used for birthdays and any yearly occasion; to a woman "w-inti saalma".
V: long life | عُقبال المِيّة | 3ugbaal il-miyya | may you reach a hundred | A warm, slightly playful wish for a long life.
R: شُكراً، وإنتَ سالِم | shukran, w-inta saalim | thanks, and may you be well
R: تِسلَم، الله يخَلّيك | tislam, alla ykhalleek | thank you, God keep you
E: عيد ميلاد سَعيد يا حَبيبي! | 3eed meelaad sa3eed ya 7abeebi! | Happy birthday, my dear!
E: كُل سَنة وإنتِ سالمة، عُقبال المِيّة | kull sana w-inti saalma, 3ugbaal il-miyya | Many happy returns, may you reach a hundred
E: إشتِرَيت لَك هَدية بعيد ميلادَك | ishtiraitlak hadiyya b-3eed meelaadak | I bought you a gift for your birthday

## good-morning
صَباح الخير | sabaa7 il-khair | morning of goodness
usage: The normal morning greeting until about noon. The reply always "goes one better" with another kind of morning: light, flowers or cream.
V: warm reply style | صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light | The classic reply, also used as a greeting.
V: Iraqi flourish | صَباح الكَيمر | sabaa7 il-gaimar | morning of clotted cream | A playful Iraqi morning greeting, named after the famous breakfast cream.
R: صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light
R: صَباح الوَرد | sabaa7 il-ward | morning of roses
E: صَباح الخير، شلونَك اليوم؟ | sabaa7 il-khair, shloonak il-yoom? | Good morning, how are you today?
E: صَباح الخير، الفُطور حاضِر | sabaa7 il-khair, il-futoor 7aadhir | Good morning, breakfast is ready
E: صَباح الخير يا جيران | sabaa7 il-khair ya jeeraan | Good morning, neighbours

## good-evening
مَسا الخير | masa il-khair | evening of goodness
usage: The greeting from late afternoon onwards. As in the morning, the reply adds a nicer word: light, jasmine or roses.
V: reply | مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light | The standard reply, also fine as a greeting.
V: very warm | مَسّاكُم الله بالخير | massaakum alla bil-khair | may God give you a good evening | A traditional, respectful greeting, often from older people.
R: مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light
R: مَسا الوَرد | masa il-ward | evening of roses
E: مَسا الخير، شكو ماكو؟ | masa il-khair, shaku maku? | Good evening, what's up?
E: مَسا الخير، تِفَضَّلوا | masa il-khair, tfadhdhalu | Good evening, please come in
E: مَسا الخير يا شَباب | masa il-khair ya shabaab | Good evening, guys

## goodbye
مَعَ السَّلامة | ma3a is-salaama | with safety
usage: The standard goodbye, said by the person staying and the person leaving. Iraqis often add "fee amaan alla" or a request to give regards to the family.
V: blessing | في أمان الله | fi amaan alla | in God's safekeeping | A warm farewell, especially to someone travelling.
V: casual | يَلّا باي | yalla baay | OK, bye | Very common among young people and on the phone.
R: الله وياك | alla wiyyaak | God be with you
R: في أمان الله | fi amaan alla | in God's safekeeping
E: يَلّا، مَعَ السَّلامة | yalla, ma3a is-salaama | OK, goodbye
E: مَعَ السَّلامة، سَلِّم على الأهل | ma3a is-salaama, sallim 3ala il-ahal | Goodbye, say hi to the family
E: الله وياك، توصَل بالسَّلامة | alla wiyyaak, toosal bis-salaama | God be with you, arrive safely

## please
رَجاءً | rajaa2an | as a request
usage: Iraqis rarely use a single word for "please". In shops and with strangers you will hear "min fadhlak" or "balla", and a request is often softened with "allah ykhalleek" (God keep you).
V: everyday | مِن فَضلَك | min fadhlak | from your kindness | Polite please to a man; to a woman "min fadhlich".
V: Iraqi | بَلّا | balla | by God | Very Iraqi, softens any request: "balla, inteeni maay" (water, please).
V: warm | الله يخَلّيك | alla ykhalleek | may God keep you | Friendly and heartfelt, used with friends and family.
R: تِدَلَّل | tdallal | at your service
R: حاضِر | 7aadhir | right away
E: بَلّا، إنطيني ماي | balla, inteeni maay | Water please
E: مِن فَضلَك، وين المَحَطّة؟ | min fadhlak, wein il-ma7atta? | Excuse me please, where is the station?
E: الله يخَلّيك، ساعِدني | alla ykhalleek, saa3idni | Please, help me

## excuse-me
لو سَمَحت | law sima7it | if you allow
usage: Use it to get attention, ask a question or pass by someone. To a woman say "law sima7ti". For bumping into someone, "il-3afu" also works.
V: to a woman | لو سِمَحتي | law sima7ti | if you allow (f) | The feminine form.
V: polite opener | عَفواً | 3afwan | pardon | Used before interrupting or asking a stranger.
R: تِفَضَّل | tfadhdhal | go ahead
R: نَعَم؟ | na3am? | yes?
E: لو سَمَحت، وين الحَمّام؟ | law sima7it, wein il-7ammaam? | Excuse me, where is the bathroom?
E: لو سَمَحت، أكو مَكان؟ | law sima7it, aku makaan? | Excuse me, is there room?
E: عَفواً، هاي السَّيّارة مالتَك؟ | 3afwan, haay is-sayyaara maaltak? | Excuse me, is this car yours?

## im-sorry
آسِف | aasif | sorry
usage: The general apology from a man; a woman says "aasfa". For small things like a bump in the street, "il-3afu" or "ma gisadit" (I didn't mean it) is more natural.
V: female speaker | آسفة | aasfa | sorry (f) | Said by a woman.
V: I didn't mean it | ما قِصَدِت | ma gisadit | I didn't intend | For accidents and misunderstandings.
V: forgive me | سامِحني | saami7ni | forgive me | For something more serious, to one man; to a woman "saam7eeni".
R: ماكو مُشكِلة | maaku mushkila | no problem
R: صار خير | saar khair | it turned out fine
E: آسِف، تأخَّرِت | aasif, t2akhkharit | Sorry, I'm late
E: آسفة، ما سِمَعتَك | aasfa, ma sima3tak | Sorry, I didn't hear you
E: سامِحني، ما قِصَدِت | saami7ni, ma gisadit | Forgive me, I didn't mean it

## yes-and-no
إي ولا | ee w-la | yes and no
usage: "Ee" is the everyday Iraqi yes and "la" the no. "Na3am" is a polite yes, and "laa wallah" is a firm no.
V: polite yes | نَعَم | na3am | yes | Respectful yes, also used to answer when called.
V: of course | طَبعاً | tab3an | of course | A confident yes.
V: firm no | لا والله | la walla | no by God | An emphatic no, or a polite refusal of an offer.
R: زين | zein | good
R: ماشي | maashi | OK
E: إي، أريد شاي | ee, areed chaay | Yes, I want tea
E: لا، شُكراً | la, shukran | No, thank you
E: إي طَبعاً، أجي وياك | ee tab3an, aji wiyyaak | Yes of course, I'll come with you

## my-name-is
إسمي | ismi | my name
usage: Say "ismi" followed by your name. Iraqis often reply with their own name and "tsharrafna" (we're honoured).
V: introducing yourself | آني إسمي | aani ismi | I, my name is | "Aani" is the Iraqi "I".
V: honoured | تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | we are honoured | The usual answer after hearing someone's name.
R: تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | nice to meet you
R: أهلاً، آني إسمي عَلي | ahlan, aani ismi 3ali | Welcome, my name is Ali
E: هَلا، إسمي سارة | hala, ismi saara | Hi, my name is Sarah
E: آني إسمي مايكِل، مِن لَندَن | aani ismi maaykil, min landan | My name is Michael, from London
E: إسمي أحمد، تشَرَّفنا | ismi a7mad, tsharrafna | My name is Ahmed, nice to meet you

## whats-your-name
شِسمَك؟ | shismak? | what's your name?
usage: The everyday question to a man; to a woman say "shismich?". "Shinu ismak?" is a slightly fuller, more careful version.
V: to a woman | شِسمِك؟ | shismich? | what's your name? (f) | The feminine form.
V: fuller | شنو إسمَك؟ | shinu ismak? | what is your name? | A little more careful, good with strangers.
R: إسمي عَلي | ismi 3ali | my name is Ali
R: آني زَينَب | aani zainab | I'm Zainab
E: هَلا، شِسمَك؟ | hala, shismak? | Hi, what's your name?
E: شِسمِك يا حِلوة؟ | shismich ya 7ilwa? | What's your name, sweetie?
E: شنو إسم إبنَك؟ | shinu isim ibnak? | What's your son's name?

## where-are-you-from
مِنين إنتَ؟ | mineen inta? | from where are you?
usage: The usual question to a man; to a woman "mineen inti?". Iraqis usually follow up by asking which city or neighbourhood.
V: to a woman | مِنين إنتِ؟ | mineen inti? | where are you from? (f) | The feminine form.
V: which city | مِن ياهو مَدينة؟ | min yaahu madeena? | from which city? | Asked after hearing the country.
R: آني مِن بَغداد | aani min baghdaad | I'm from Baghdad
R: مِن البَصرة | min il-basra | from Basra
E: مِنين إنتَ؟ إنتَ مو عِراقي | mineen inta? inta mu 3iraagi | Where are you from? You're not Iraqi
E: آني مِن أمريكا، وإنتَ؟ | aani min amreeka, w-inta? | I'm from America, and you?
E: أهلي مِن الموصِل | ahli min il-moosil | My family is from Mosul

## i-dont-understand
ما أفتِهِم | ma aftihim | I don't understand
usage: The Iraqi way to say you don't understand. Add "3eed" (repeat) or "shwayya shwayya" (slowly) to get help.
V: repeat please | عيد مِن فَضلَك | 3eed min fadhlak | repeat please | Ask someone to say it again.
V: slower | شوَيّة شوَيّة | shwayya shwayya | little by little | Ask someone to speak slowly.
R: زين، أعيدها | zein, a3eedha | OK, I'll repeat it
R: ماكو مُشكِلة | maaku mushkila | no problem
E: آسِف، ما أفتِهِم | aasif, ma aftihim | Sorry, I don't understand
E: ما أفتِهِم، عيد شوَيّة شوَيّة | ma aftihim, 3eed shwayya shwayya | I don't understand, repeat slowly
E: ما فِتهَمِت شنو قِلِت | ma fithamit shinu gilit | I didn't understand what you said

## do-you-speak-english
تِحكي إنكليزي؟ | ti7chi ingleezi? | do you speak English?
usage: Ask a man with "ti7chi"; ask a woman "ti7cheen ingleezi?". Many young Iraqis speak some English and are happy to help.
V: to a woman | تِحكين إنكليزي؟ | ti7cheen ingleezi? | do you speak English? (f) | The feminine form.
V: a little | شوَيّة | shwayya | a little | A common answer.
R: إي، شوَيّة | ee, shwayya | yes, a little
R: لا، بَس عَرَبي | la, bas 3arabi | no, only Arabic
E: لو سَمَحت، تِحكي إنكليزي؟ | law sima7it, ti7chi ingleezi? | Excuse me, do you speak English?
E: آني أحكي عَرَبي شوَيّة | aani a7chi 3arabi shwayya | I speak a little Arabic
E: أكو واحِد يِحكي إنكليزي هنا؟ | aku waa7id yi7chi ingleezi hna? | Is there someone who speaks English here?

## how-much-is-this
بيش هاذا؟ | beesh haadha? | for what is this?
usage: The classic Iraqi shopping question. "Beesh" means "for how much". Bargaining is normal in the markets.
V: price | شكَد سِعرَه؟ | shgadd si3ra? | how much is its price? | Another common way to ask.
V: bargaining | يِرخَص شوَيّة؟ | yirkhas shwayya? | does it get a bit cheaper? | Opening line for a discount.
R: بعَشرة آلاف | b-3ashrat aalaaf | ten thousand
R: إلَك بخَمسة | ilak b-khamsa | for you, five
E: بيش هاذا القَميص؟ | beesh haadha il-gamees? | How much is this shirt?
E: غالي! يِرخَص شوَيّة؟ | ghaali! yirkhas shwayya? | Expensive! Can it be cheaper?
E: شكَد كيلو الطَّماطة؟ | shgadd keelu it-tamaata? | How much is a kilo of tomatoes?

## i-love-you
أحِبَّك | a7ibbak | I love you
usage: To a man "a7ibbak", to a woman "a7ibbich". Iraqis also use rich terms of endearment like "3yooni" (my eyes) and "galbi" (my heart).
V: to a woman | أحِبِّك | a7ibbich | I love you (f) | The feminine form.
V: affection | إنتَ عيوني | inta 3yooni | you are my eyes | Very common Iraqi endearment.
V: deep | أموت عَليك | amoot 3aleek | I die for you | Strong, romantic.
R: وآني أحِبَّك | w-aani a7ibbak | and I love you
R: وآني أكثَر | w-aani akthar | and I more
E: أحِبَّك هوايه | a7ibbak hwaaya | I love you so much
E: ماما، أحِبِّك | maama, a7ibbich | Mum, I love you
E: إنتِ عيوني وقَلبي | inti 3yooni w-galbi | You're my eyes and my heart

## congratulations
مَبروك | mabrook | blessed
usage: For any happy news: a new job, a baby, a house, a wedding. The reply is "allah ybaarik bik" (God bless you).
V: more | ألف مَبروك | alf mabrook | a thousand congratulations | Extra warm.
V: wedding | مَبروك، بالرّفاه والبَنين | mabrook, bir-rifaah wil-baneen | with harmony and sons | Traditional wish for newlyweds.
R: الله يبارِك بيك | alla ybaarik beek | God bless you
R: عُقبالَك | 3ugbaalak | may it be your turn
E: مَبروك الوَظيفة الجِديدة! | mabrook il-wadheefa ij-jideeda! | Congratulations on the new job!
E: ألف مَبروك على البيت | alf mabrook 3ala il-beit | Congratulations on the house
E: مَبروك، عُقبال الفَرحة الجاية | mabrook, 3ugbaal il-far7a ij-jaaya | Congratulations, to the next celebration

## welcome
أهلاً وسَهلاً | ahlan w-sahlan | family and ease
usage: Said to welcome a guest. Iraqi hospitality is famous, and a welcome is usually followed by tea and food.
V: warm | هَلا بيك | hala beek | welcome to you | Friendly welcome to one man.
V: come in | تِفَضَّل | tfadhdhal | please | Inviting someone in or to sit.
R: أهلاً بيك | ahlan beek | welcome to you
R: الله يحَيّيك | alla y7ayyeek | God greet you
E: أهلاً وسَهلاً، تِفَضَّل | ahlan w-sahlan, tfadhdhal | Welcome, come in
E: هَلا بيكُم بالعِراق | hala beekum bil-3iraag | Welcome to Iraq
E: البيت بيتَك | il-beit beitak | My house is your house

## coffee
قَهوة | gahwa | coffee
usage: Iraqis drink tea far more often, but coffee is everywhere in cafés. Bitter Arabic coffee (gahwa 3arabiyya) is served to guests and at funerals.
V: Arabic coffee | قَهوة عَرَبية | gahwa 3arabiyya | Arabic coffee | Bitter, cardamom coffee in small cups.
V: Turkish coffee | قَهوة تُركية | gahwa turkiyya | Turkish coffee | Thick coffee, common in cafés.
R: شلون تريدها؟ | shloon treedha? | how do you want it?
R: بلا شَكَر | bila shakar | without sugar
E: أريد قَهوة مِن فَضلَك | areed gahwa min fadhlak | I'd like a coffee please
E: القَهوة بَلا شَكَر | il-gahwa bala shakar | Coffee without sugar
E: تشرب قَهوة لو شاي؟ | tishrab gahwa lo chaay? | Do you drink coffee or tea?

## family
عائلة | 3aa2ila | family
usage: "3aa2ila" is the family; "ahal" is your people or relatives. Family is central in Iraq, and people often ask about each other's families.
V: relatives | أهل | ahal | folks | Your parents and wider family.
V: household | بيت | beit | house | "Beit abu 3ali" means Ali's father's family.
R: الحَمدِلله، كُلهُم زينين | il-7amdilla, kullhum zeineen | thank God, they're all fine
R: يسَلمون عليك | ysallmoon 3aleek | they send their regards
E: عِندي عائلة كبيرة | 3indi 3aa2ila chbeera | I have a big family
E: شلون الأهل؟ | shloon il-ahal? | How's the family?
E: أهلي ساكنين ببَغداد | ahli saakneen b-baghdaad | My family lives in Baghdad

## whats-the-weather-like
شلون الجَو؟ | shloon ij-jaww? | how is the weather?
usage: Weather talk in Iraq is mostly about heat in summer and dust storms. "Ij-jaww" is the weather.
V: today | شلون الجَو اليوم؟ | shloon ij-jaww il-yoom? | how's the weather today? | Asking about today.
V: hot | الدُّنيا حَر | id-dinya 7arr | the world is hot | The most common summer answer.
R: حار كُلِّش | 7aarr kullish | very hot
R: أكو عَجاج | aku 3ajaaj | there's a dust storm
E: شلون الجَو ببَغداد؟ | shloon ij-jaww b-baghdaad? | What's the weather like in Baghdad?
E: الجَو حِلو بالشِّتا | ij-jaww 7ilu bish-shita | The weather is nice in winter
E: اليوم ماكو مُطَر | il-yoom maaku mutar | Today there's no rain

## im-busy
آني مَشغول | aani mashghool | I am busy
usage: A man says "mashghool", a woman "mashghoola". Iraqis often soften it with an apology or a promise to call back.
V: female speaker | آني مَشغولة | aani mashghoola | I'm busy (f) | Said by a woman.
V: right now | هَسّة ما أقدَر | hassa ma agdar | now I can't | "Hassa" is the Iraqi "now".
R: ماكو مُشكِلة | maaku mushkila | no problem
R: زين، أخابرَك بَعدين | zein, akhaabrak ba3dain | OK, I'll call you later
E: آسِف، آني مَشغول هَسّة | aasif, aani mashghool hassa | Sorry, I'm busy now
E: مَشغولة بالشُّغُل | mashghoola bish-shughul | I'm busy with work
E: إذا مو مَشغول، تَعال | idha mu mashghool, ta3aal | If you're not busy, come

## see-you-later
أشوفَك بَعدين | ashoofak ba3dain | I see you later
usage: To a man "ashoofak", to a woman "ashoofich". Often said with "alla wiyyaak" or "yalla".
V: tomorrow | أشوفَك باجِر | ashoofak baachir | see you tomorrow | "Baachir" is the Iraqi "tomorrow".
V: to a woman | أشوفِك بَعدين | ashoofich ba3dain | see you later (f) | The feminine form.
R: إن شاء الله | in shaa2 alla | God willing
R: الله وياك | alla wiyyaak | God be with you
E: يَلّا، أشوفَك بَعدين | yalla, ashoofak ba3dain | OK, see you later
E: أشوفَك باجِر بالشُّغُل | ashoofak baachir bish-shughul | See you tomorrow at work
E: مَعَ السَّلامة، أشوفكُم بالعيد | ma3a is-salaama, ashoofkum bil-3eed | Bye, see you all at Eid
