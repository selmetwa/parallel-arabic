# Khaleeji (Emirati/Kuwaiti) phrasebook. Standard letters only; ج said "y", ك sometimes "ch", ق said "g" in the transliteration.

## hello
هَلا | hala | welcome
usage: The everyday Gulf hello, warm and fine for anyone. Greetings in the Gulf are long: after "hala" come questions about health, family and news, often repeated.
V: formal / traditional | السَّلامُ عَلَيكُم | is-salaamu 3alaikum | peace be upon you | The standard greeting when entering a majlis, a shop or an office; always answered with "w-3alaikum is-salaam".
V: extra warm | هَلا والله | hala walla | welcome, by God | Very common and warm, especially when meeting a friend.
V: casual | هايّ | haay | hi | Borrowed from English, common among young people in Dubai and Kuwait.
R: هَلا فيك | hala feek | welcome to you
R: هَلا والله، حَيّاك | hala walla, 7ayyaak | welcome, God greet you
E: هَلا، شلونَك اليوم؟ | hala, shloonak il-yoom? | Hello, how are you today?
E: هَلا والله، شو الأخبار؟ | hala walla, shu il-akhbaar? | Hello, what's new?
E: هَلا، أحمد موجود؟ | hala, a7mad mawjood? | Hello, is Ahmed here?

## how-are-you
شلونَك؟ | shloonak? | what is your colour?
usage: The standard Gulf "how are you?" to one man; to a woman "shloonich?", to a group "shloonkum?". Emiratis also say "kaifak?". It is normal to ask more than once.
V: to a woman | شلونِك؟ | shloonich? | how are you? (f) | The feminine form.
V: Emirati | كيف حالَك؟ | kaif 7aalak? | how is your state? | Common in the UAE, slightly more formal.
V: news | شو العِلم؟ | shu il-3ilm? | what's the news? | A very Gulf way to ask what's new.
R: بخير، الحَمدُلله | b-khair, il-7amdilla | well, praise God
R: زين، وإنتَ شلونَك؟ | zain, w-inta shloonak? | good, and how are you?
E: هَلا خالِد، شلونَك؟ | hala khaalid, shloonak? | Hi Khalid, how are you?
E: شلونِك يا يُمّا؟ | shloonich ya yumma? | How are you, mum?
E: شلون الأهل والعيال؟ | shloon il-ahal wil-3yaal? | How are the family and kids?

## thank-you
مَشكور | mashkoor | thanked
usage: The usual Gulf thank you to a man; to a woman "mashkoora". "Shukran" is fine too. Gulf people often answer thanks with a blessing.
V: to a woman | مَشكورة | mashkoora | thank you (f) | The feminine form.
V: blessing | يَزاك الله خير | yazaak alla khair | may God reward you | Warm, very common in the Gulf.
V: to someone who served you | تِسلَم إيدَك | tislam eedak | bless your hand | After a meal or a favour.
R: العَفو | il-3afu | you're welcome
R: ما سَوَّينا شي | ma sawwaina shay | we did nothing
E: مَشكور على الضّيافة | mashkoor 3ala idh-dhiyaafa | Thanks for the hospitality
E: يَزاك الله خير، ما قَصَّرت | yazaak alla khair, ma gassart | God reward you, you did plenty
E: شُكراً، الأكل وايد زين | shukran, il-akil waayid zain | Thanks, the food is very good

## happy-birthday
عيد ميلاد سَعيد | 3eed meelaad sa3eed | happy feast of birth
usage: The standard birthday wish. Gulf people add "kill 3aam w-inta b-khair" (every year and you are well), the same wish used for Eid.
V: yearly blessing | كِل عام وإنتَ بخير | kill 3aam w-inta b-khair | every year and you are well | For birthdays and any yearly occasion.
V: long life | عُقبال المِيّة | 3ugbaal il-miyya | may you reach a hundred | A playful wish for a long life.
R: وإنتَ بخير | w-inta b-khair | and you are well
R: مَشكور، الله يخَلّيك | mashkoor, alla ykhalleek | thanks, God keep you
E: عيد ميلاد سَعيد يا الغالي! | 3eed meelaad sa3eed ya il-ghaali! | Happy birthday, dear one!
E: كِل عام وإنتِ بخير، عُقبال المِيّة | kill 3aam w-inti b-khair, 3ugbaal il-miyya | Many happy returns, may you reach a hundred
E: يِبتَلَك هَدية | yibtalak hadiyya | I brought you a gift

## good-morning
صَباح الخير | sabaa7 il-khair | morning of goodness
usage: The normal morning greeting. As elsewhere, the reply adds a nicer morning: light, roses or jasmine.
V: reply | صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light | The classic reply.
V: very warm | صَبَّحَك الله بالخير | sabba7ak alla bil-khair | may God give you a good morning | Traditional, respectful.
R: صَباح النّور | sabaa7 in-noor | morning of light
R: صَباح الوَرد | sabaa7 il-ward | morning of roses
E: صَباح الخير، شلونَك اليوم؟ | sabaa7 il-khair, shloonak il-yoom? | Good morning, how are you today?
E: صَباح الخير، الرّيوق جاهِز | sabaa7 il-khair, ir-ryoog yaahiz | Good morning, breakfast is ready
E: صَباح الخير يا يِيران | sabaa7 il-khair ya yeeraan | Good morning, neighbours

## good-evening
مَسا الخير | masa il-khair | evening of goodness
usage: The greeting from late afternoon on. The reply adds a nicer word.
V: reply | مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light | The standard reply.
V: traditional | مَسّاك الله بالخير | massaak alla bil-khair | may God give you a good evening | Respectful, used by older people.
R: مَسا النّور | masa in-noor | evening of light
R: مَسا الوَرد | masa il-ward | evening of roses
E: مَسا الخير، شو العِلم؟ | masa il-khair, shu il-3ilm? | Good evening, what's new?
E: مَسا الخير، تِفَضَّلوا | masa il-khair, tfadhdhalu | Good evening, please come in
E: مَسا الخير يا الرّبع | masa il-khair ya ir-rab3 | Good evening, guys

## goodbye
مَعَ السَّلامة | ma3a is-salaama | with safety
usage: The standard goodbye. Gulf people often add "fi amaan alla" or a request to greet the family.
V: blessing | في أمان الله | fi amaan alla | in God's safekeeping | Warm, especially for travellers.
V: casual | يَلّا باي | yalla baay | OK, bye | Very common among young people.
R: الله يِسَلمَك | alla ysallimak | God keep you safe
R: في أمان الله | fi amaan alla | in God's safekeeping
E: يَلّا، مَعَ السَّلامة | yalla, ma3a is-salaama | OK, goodbye
E: مَعَ السَّلامة، سَلِّم على الأهل | ma3a is-salaama, sallim 3ala il-ahal | Goodbye, greet the family
E: توصَل بالسَّلامة | toosal bis-salaama | Arrive safely

## please
لو سَمَحت | law sama7t | if you allow
usage: The everyday please when asking for something. "Min fadhlak" is a bit more formal, and "alla ykhalleek" is warm and personal.
V: formal | مِن فَضلَك | min fadhlak | from your kindness | Polite, for shops and offices.
V: warm | الله يخَلّيك | alla ykhalleek | may God keep you | Friendly and heartfelt.
V: to a woman | لو سَمَحتي | law sama7ti | please (f) | The feminine form.
R: تِأمُر | tu2mur | at your command
R: حاضِر | 7aadhir | right away
E: لو سَمَحت، عَطني ماي | law sama7t, 3atni maay | Water please
E: مِن فَضلَك، وين المِترو؟ | min fadhlak, wain il-mitro? | Excuse me please, where is the metro?
E: الله يخَلّيك، ساعِدني | alla ykhalleek, saa3idni | Please, help me

## excuse-me
لو سَمَحت | law sama7t | if you allow
usage: Use it to get attention or ask a stranger. To a woman "law sama7ti". "Il-3afu" works for bumping into someone.
V: pardon | عَفواً | 3afwan | pardon | Before interrupting.
V: let me pass | بالإذِن | bil-idhin | with permission | When passing between people.
R: تِفَضَّل | tfadhdhal | go ahead
R: هَلا، آمِر | hala, aamir | yes, at your service
E: لو سَمَحت، وين الحَمّام؟ | law sama7t, wain il-7ammaam? | Excuse me, where is the bathroom?
E: بالإذِن، أبي أمُر | bil-idhin, abi amurr | Excuse me, I want to pass
E: عَفواً، هاي السَّيّارة مالَك؟ | 3afwan, haay is-sayyaara maalak? | Excuse me, is this car yours?

## im-sorry
آسِف | aasif | sorry
usage: The general apology from a man; a woman says "aasfa". "Sma7li" (forgive me) is very common and natural.
V: female speaker | آسفة | aasfa | sorry (f) | Said by a woman.
V: forgive me | سامِحني | saami7ni | forgive me | For something more serious.
V: excuse me | سَمحلي | sma7li | permit me | Light, everyday apology.
R: عادي، ولا يهِمَّك | 3aadi, wala yhimmak | normal, don't worry
R: ما صار شي | ma saar shay | nothing happened
E: آسِف، تأخَّرت | aasif, t2akhkhart | Sorry, I'm late
E: آسفة، ما سِمَعتَك | aasfa, ma sima3tak | Sorry, I didn't hear you
E: سامِحني، ما قِصَدت | saami7ni, ma gisadt | Forgive me, I didn't mean it

## yes-and-no
إي ولا | ee w-la | yes and no
usage: "Ee" or "aywa" is the everyday yes, "la" the no. "Na3am" is a polite yes. "Abshir" (good news) is a Gulf way of saying "of course, I'll do it".
V: polite yes | نَعَم | na3am | yes | Respectful, also to answer when called.
V: of course | أبشِر | abshir | rejoice | Gulf "sure, consider it done".
V: firm no | لا والله | la walla | no by God | Emphatic no, or polite refusal of an offer.
R: زين | zain | good
R: تَمام | tamaam | fine
E: إي، أبي شاي | ee, abi chaay | Yes, I want tea
E: لا، مَشكور | la, mashkoor | No, thanks
E: أبشِر، بَجي وياك | abshir, bayi wiyyaak | Sure, I'll come with you

## my-name-is
إسمي | ismi | my name
usage: Say "ismi" then your name. The reply is often "tsharrafna" (we're honoured).
V: introducing yourself | أنا إسمي | ana ismi | I, my name is | Fuller form.
V: honoured | تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | we are honoured | The usual answer.
R: تشَرَّفنا | tsharrafna | nice to meet you
R: هَلا، أنا إسمي سالِم | hala, ana ismi saalim | hi, my name is Salem
E: هَلا، إسمي سارة | hala, ismi saara | Hi, my name is Sarah
E: أنا إسمي مايكِل، مِن لَندَن | ana ismi maaykil, min landan | My name is Michael, from London
E: إسمي مَريَم، تشَرَّفنا | ismi maryam, tsharrafna | My name is Maryam, nice to meet you

## whats-your-name
شِسمَك؟ | shismak? | what's your name?
usage: The usual question to a man; to a woman "shismich?". "Shu ismak?" is used in the UAE.
V: to a woman | شِسمِك؟ | shismich? | what's your name? (f) | The feminine form.
V: Emirati | شو إسمَك؟ | shu ismak? | what is your name? | Common in the UAE.
R: إسمي سالِم | ismi saalim | my name is Salem
R: أنا نورة | ana noora | I'm Noura
E: هَلا، شِسمَك؟ | hala, shismak? | Hi, what's your name?
E: شِسمِك يا بِنتي؟ | shismich ya binti? | What's your name, dear?
E: شو إسم وِلدَك؟ | shu isim wildak? | What's your son's name?

## where-are-you-from
مِنين إنتَ؟ | mneen inta? | from where are you?
usage: To a man "mneen inta?", to a woman "mneen inti?". In the Gulf, with many expats, this is a very common question.
V: to a woman | مِنين إنتِ؟ | mneen inti? | where are you from? (f) | The feminine form.
V: which emirate | مِن أي إمارة؟ | min ay imaara? | from which emirate? | Asked of Emiratis.
R: أنا مِن دُبَي | ana min dubai | I'm from Dubai
R: مِن الكويت | min il-kuwait | from Kuwait
E: مِنين إنتَ؟ إنتَ مو خَليجي | mneen inta? inta mu khaleeji | Where are you from? You're not Gulf
E: أنا مِن أمريكا، وإنتَ؟ | ana min amreeka, w-inta? | I'm from America, and you?
E: أهلي مِن العين | ahli min il-3ain | My family is from Al Ain

## i-dont-understand
ما فِهَمت | ma fihamt | I didn't understand
usage: The natural Gulf way to say you don't understand. Add "3eed" (repeat) or "shway shway" (slowly).
V: repeat please | عيد لو سَمَحت | 3eed law sama7t | repeat please | Ask someone to say it again.
V: slower | شوَي شوَي | shway shway | little by little | Ask someone to speak slowly.
R: زين، أعيدها | zain, a3eedha | OK, I'll repeat it
R: عادي | 3aadi | no problem
E: آسِف، ما فِهَمت | aasif, ma fihamt | Sorry, I didn't understand
E: ما فِهَمت، عيد شوَي شوَي | ma fihamt, 3eed shway shway | I didn't understand, repeat slowly
E: ما فِهَمت شو قِلت | ma fihamt shu gilt | I didn't understand what you said

## do-you-speak-english
تِتكَلَّم إنجليزي؟ | titkallam ingleezi? | do you speak English?
usage: To a man "titkallam", to a woman "titkallamin". English is widely spoken in the Gulf, especially in Dubai.
V: to a woman | تِتكَلَّمين إنجليزي؟ | titkallameen ingleezi? | do you speak English? (f) | The feminine form.
V: a little | شوَي | shway | a little | A common answer.
R: إي، شوَي | ee, shway | yes, a little
R: أكيد | akeed | of course
E: لو سَمَحت، تِتكَلَّم إنجليزي؟ | law sama7t, titkallam ingleezi? | Excuse me, do you speak English?
E: أنا أتكَلَّم عَرَبي شوَي | ana atkallam 3arabi shway | I speak a little Arabic
E: في أحَد يِتكَلَّم إنجليزي هني؟ | fi a7ad yitkallam ingleezi hni? | Is there anyone who speaks English here?

## how-much-is-this
بِكَم هاذا؟ | bikam haadha? | for how much is this?
usage: The everyday shopping question. "Cham?" (how much) is also common. Bargaining is normal in souks, not in malls.
V: price | كَم سِعرَه؟ | cham si3ra? | how much is its price? | Very common; Kuwaitis say "cham".
V: discount | في خَصم؟ | fi khasm? | is there a discount? | Opening line for bargaining.
R: بِعَشر دَراهِم | b-3ashar daraahim | ten dirhams
R: لك بِسَبعة | lak b-sab3a | for you, seven
E: بِكَم هاذا الثّوب؟ | bikam haadha ith-thoob? | How much is this robe?
E: غالي! في خَصم؟ | ghaali! fi khasm? | Expensive! Is there a discount?
E: بِكَم كيلو الرُّطَب؟ | bikam keelu ir-rutab? | How much is a kilo of fresh dates?

## i-love-you
أحِبَّك | a7ibbak | I love you
usage: To a man "a7ibbak", to a woman "a7ibbich". Gulf endearments include "ya galbi" (my heart) and "ya 3umri" (my life).
V: to a woman | أحِبِّك | a7ibbich | I love you (f) | The feminine form.
V: affection | يا قَلبي | ya galbi | my heart | Very common endearment.
V: deep | أموت فيك | amoot feek | I die in you | Strong, romantic.
R: وأنا أحِبَّك | w-ana a7ibbak | and I love you
R: وأنا أكثَر | w-ana akthar | and I more
E: أحِبَّك وايد | a7ibbak waayid | I love you so much
E: يُمّا، أحِبِّك | yumma, a7ibbich | Mum, I love you
E: إنتِ عُمري | inti 3umri | You're my life

## congratulations
مَبروك | mabrook | blessed
usage: For any happy news. The reply is "alla ybaarik feek" (God bless you).
V: more | ألف مَبروك | alf mabrook | a thousand congratulations | Extra warm.
V: new baby | الحَمدُلله على السَّلامة | il-7amdilla 3ala is-salaama | thank God for safety | Said to a new mother.
R: الله يبارِك فيك | alla ybaarik feek | God bless you
R: عُقبالَك | 3ugbaalak | may it be your turn
E: مَبروك الوَظيفة اليِديدة! | mabrook il-wadheefa il-yideeda! | Congratulations on the new job!
E: ألف مَبروك على البيت | alf mabrook 3ala il-bait | Congratulations on the house
E: مَبروك، عُقبال العِرس | mabrook, 3ugbaal il-3irs | Congratulations, may the wedding come next

## welcome
حَيّاك الله | 7ayyaak alla | may God greet you
usage: The classic Gulf welcome to a guest, to one man; to a woman "7ayyaach alla", to a group "7ayyaakum alla". Coffee and dates always follow.
V: to a group | حَيّاكُم الله | 7ayyaakum alla | welcome all | For several guests.
V: standard | أهلاً وسَهلاً | ahlan w-sahlan | family and ease | Understood everywhere.
R: الله يحَيّيك | alla y7ayyeek | God greet you
R: الله يِسَلمَك | alla ysallimak | God keep you safe
E: حَيّاك الله، تِفَضَّل | 7ayyaak alla, tfadhdhal | Welcome, come in
E: حَيّاكُم الله في الإمارات | 7ayyaakum alla fil-imaaraat | Welcome to the UAE
E: البيت بيتَك | il-bait baitak | My house is your house

## coffee
قَهوة | gahwa | coffee
usage: Arabic coffee (gahwa) is the heart of Gulf hospitality: light, with cardamom and saffron, poured from a dalla into small cups. Shake the cup gently when you've had enough.
V: Arabic coffee | قَهوة عَرَبية | gahwa 3arabiyya | Arabic coffee | Served to guests with dates.
V: enough | بَس، مَشكور | bas, mashkoor | enough, thanks | Said while shaking the cup.
R: تِبي قَهوة؟ | tabi gahwa? | do you want coffee?
R: سُكَّر ولا بِدون؟ | sukkar walla bidoon? | sugar or without?
E: أبي قَهوة لو سَمَحت | abi gahwa law sama7t | I'd like a coffee please
E: القَهوة العَرَبية مَعَ تَمر | il-gahwa il-3arabiyya ma3a tamir | Arabic coffee with dates
E: تِشرَب قَهوة ولا شاي؟ | tishrab gahwa walla chaay? | Do you drink coffee or tea?

## family
أهل | ahal | family
usage: "Ahal" means family and relatives; "3yaal" are the kids. Family gatherings, often weekly, are central to Gulf life.
V: kids | عيال | 3yaal | kids | Your children.
V: household | بيت | bait | house | "Bait il-3ood" is the grandparents' house where the family gathers.
R: الحَمدُلله، كِلهُم بخير | il-7amdilla, kilhum b-khair | thank God, all are well
R: يِسَلمون عَلَيك | ysallmoon 3alaik | they send their regards
E: عِندي أهل وايد | 3indi ahal waayid | I have a big family
E: شلون الأهل؟ | shloon il-ahal? | How's the family?
E: كِل يُمعة نِتيَمَّع في بيت يَدّي | kill yum3a nityamma3 fi bait yaddi | Every Friday we gather at grandpa's house

## whats-the-weather-like
شلون الجَو؟ | shloon il-yaw? | how is the weather?
usage: In the Gulf, weather talk is mostly about heat, humidity and sand. Winter is the pleasant season.
V: today | شلون الجَو اليوم؟ | shloon il-yaw il-yoom? | how's the weather today? | Asking about today.
V: humid | رُطوبة | rtooba | humidity | A constant summer topic.
R: حَر وايد | 7arr waayid | very hot
R: الجَو حِلو | il-yaw 7ilu | the weather is nice
E: شلون الجَو في دُبَي؟ | shloon il-yaw fi dubai? | What's the weather like in Dubai?
E: الجَو حِلو في الشِّتا | il-yaw 7ilu fish-shita | The weather is nice in winter
E: اليوم في غُبار | il-yoom fi ghbaar | Today there's dust

## im-busy
أنا مَشغول | ana mashghool | I am busy
usage: A man says "mashghool", a woman "mashghoola". It is softened with an apology or a promise to call back.
V: female speaker | أنا مَشغولة | ana mashghoola | I'm busy (f) | Said by a woman.
V: right now | الحين ما أقدَر | il-7een ma agdar | now I can't | "Il-7een" is the Gulf "now".
R: عادي | 3aadi | no problem
R: زين، أتِّصِل فيك بَعدين | zain, attisil feek ba3dain | OK, I'll call you later
E: آسِف، أنا مَشغول الحين | aasif, ana mashghool il-7een | Sorry, I'm busy now
E: مَشغولة بالدَّوام | mashghoola bid-dawaam | I'm busy with work
E: إذا مو مَشغول، تَعال | idha mu mashghool, ta3aal | If you're not busy, come

## see-you-later
أشوفَك بَعدين | ashoofak ba3dain | I see you later
usage: To a man "ashoofak", to a woman "ashoofich". Often with "in shaa2 alla".
V: tomorrow | أشوفَك باكِر | ashoofak baachir | see you tomorrow | "Baachir" is the Gulf "tomorrow".
V: to a woman | أشوفِك بَعدين | ashoofich ba3dain | see you later (f) | The feminine form.
R: إن شاء الله | in shaa2 alla | God willing
R: في أمان الله | fi amaan alla | in God's safekeeping
E: يَلّا، أشوفَك بَعدين | yalla, ashoofak ba3dain | OK, see you later
E: أشوفَك باكِر في الدَّوام | ashoofak baachir fid-dawaam | See you tomorrow at work
E: مَعَ السَّلامة، أشوفكُم في العيد | ma3a is-salaama, ashoofkum fil-3eed | Bye, see you all at Eid
